# Core Interview Synthesis

## Purpose

This document provides a consistent structure for comparing completed and remaining core validation interviews. PILOT-A1, INT-A2, and INT-A3 are recorded; INT-A4 is the next confirmed core interview; and INT-A5 is reserved for an optional replacement participant who has not yet been selected. After INT-A4, the project may decide whether another core interview is required. The synthesis must not be used to treat isolated reports as general facts or to authorize implementation prematurely.

## Current hypothesis under test

The current revised hypothesis is a private, mobile-friendly organizer cockpit for martial-arts seminars, built around reusable seminar playbooks, a long-range timeline, clear responsibility and completion confirmation, private contingency planning, promotion and merchandise checkpoints, guest logistics, and post-event learning, while urgent communication remains in existing phone and chat tools. The organizer keeps the complete picture, while collaborators receive only the tasks and confirmations relevant to them.

This is a hypothesis to test, not a validated concept or selected final solution.

## Evidence labels

- **[OE] Observed evidence** — behavior or an artifact directly observed during research. No such evidence is currently recorded for PILOT-A1, INT-A2, or INT-A3.
- **[PR] Participant report** — the participant's account of a past process, incident, or behavior.
- **[PO] Participant opinion** — the participant's preference, judgment, prediction, or reaction to a concept.
- **[RI] Researcher inference** — an interpretation derived from the interview records; it remains a hypothesis until separately tested.
- **Pending interview** — no evidence has been collected for that participant code.
- **Replacement participant not yet selected** — INT-A5 is optional, and no replacement participant has been selected.

## Decision gate

This core-interview gate decides only whether a narrow product hypothesis is ready for the next validation stage. After INT-A4, the project must decide whether the available evidence is sufficient or an optional INT-A5 replacement interview is required. The gate does not authorize application code, architecture, or full implementation. Broader validation requirements remain applicable.

**Current overall status: `REVISE / CONTINUE VALIDATION`.** INT-A3 strengthens the target niche and revises the working hypothesis toward a private organizer cockpit. INT-A4 remains pending, while an INT-A5 replacement participant has not yet been selected. After INT-A4, the project may decide whether another core interview is required. No prototype has been tested, and willingness to pay remains untested.

### PROCEED

Proceed to a bounded low-fidelity validation stage only when:

- at least three completed core interviews contain a specific relevant past incident with an observable consequence;
- a coherent primary user and recurring event workflow are supported by more than one interview;
- the proposed direction addresses a gap not already handled sufficiently by current tools;
- at least two participants identify conditions under which they would use the specialized approach;
- a plausible person responsible for maintaining the information is identified;
- major counter-evidence and adoption barriers have a testable response;
- the decision selects a narrow hypothesis, not a generic event-management platform.

### REVISE

Revise the problem, audience, or value proposition when:

- the coordination problem repeats, but evidence supports different event categories or users;
- incidents are credible, but existing tools appear sufficient for most participants;
- maintenance burden, duplicated updates, or unclear ownership remain unresolved;
- participants value templates, reminders, or guided risk checks but not the current mechanism;
- supporting and contradicting evidence are too balanced for a defensible direction.

Define one revision and test it with new evidence before reconsidering `PROCEED`.

### KILL

Kill the current product hypothesis when:

- fewer than three completed core interviews provide a specific relevant past incident;
- reported consequences are consistently minor and do not justify a separate product;
- most participants report that current tools are sufficient and cannot identify differentiated value;
- no credible primary user or maintainer emerges;
- likely users reject the required update effort even for the narrowest useful workflow;
- the remaining concept is only generic task management, a thin AI wrapper, or a duplicate of existing tools.

Killing the current hypothesis does not erase valid evidence about the broader coordination problem; it prevents unsupported implementation of this solution.
