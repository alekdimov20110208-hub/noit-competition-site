# Interview INT-A3 Decision

## Decision

`REVISE`

The specialized martial-arts seminar direction receives stronger support, but the working hypothesis must be revised from a broadly shared team workspace toward a private organizer cockpit with lightweight collaborator interaction.

This is an interview-level decision from one high-relevance participant. It is not an overall project `PROCEED` decision or approval to implement.

## What was supported

The following findings are supported for this participant and the seminar process described:

- **[PR]** International martial-arts seminar preparation followed a recurring model with stable reference points.
- **[PR]** The process operated over a long timeline, including approval, invitation, confirmation, announcement, travel, promotion, logistics, and schedule preparation.
- **[PR]** Approximately 98% of information, overall coordination, decisions, and timely activation depended on the chief organizer, while execution was distributed among helpers.
- **[PR]** Tasks were assigned mainly orally, and completion was often assumed through trust rather than systematically confirmed in writing.
- **[PR]** The chief organizer remained the reminder and proactive checking point for time-sensitive work.
- **[PR]** Backup plans were concentrated mainly in the chief organizer's memory.
- **[PR]** A delayed instructor was covered by a preplanned substitute, protecting the public schedule.
- **[PR]** Promotion, merchandise, some deadlines, and systematic capture of material for future promotion remained weak despite positive visitor experience.
- **[PO]** Phone and chat were valued for immediate feedback.
- **[PO]** Private organizer know-how and limited collaborator access appeared more suitable than distributing the complete playbook to everyone.

These findings remain participant-level evidence and must not be generalized to all martial-arts organizers.

## What was not validated

INT-A3 does not validate:

- a general-purpose event platform;
- replacing telephone, Viber, WhatsApp, or other urgent communication channels;
- requiring every helper to use a complex application;
- public visibility of every contingency;
- a complex visual dependency graph as a desired representation;
- automatic simulation as the core product value;
- a fully self-service playbook suitable for every organizer without adaptation;
- willingness to pay;
- repeat use across independent organizations;
- prototype usability or task-confirmation behavior;
- the participant's future-use predictions as behavioral proof.

## Revised hypothesis to continue testing

> A private, mobile-friendly organizer cockpit for martial-arts seminars, built around reusable seminar playbooks, a long-range timeline, clear responsibility and completion confirmation, private contingency planning, promotion and merchandise checkpoints, guest logistics, and post-event learning, while urgent communication remains in existing phone and chat tools.

The team-facing component should be lightweight:

- the organizer keeps the complete picture;
- collaborators receive only the tasks and confirmations relevant to them;
- private contingency information is restricted to the organizer and selected trusted people.

This is a revised hypothesis under test, not a selected final solution or approved implementation scope.

## Main product implications to validate

Candidate requirements receiving stronger participant-level support include:

- reusable seminar templates and a long-range preparation timeline;
- an organizer cockpit with responsibility, deadline, completion, and red/green status visibility;
- selective reminders rather than a requirement for constant team-wide updates;
- private contingency notes, backup owners, and restricted visibility;
- guest arrival, departure, transport, accommodation, and preference planning;
- schedule approval and protection;
- promotion and merchandise checkpoints;
- venue alternatives and capacity checks;
- post-event lessons that improve later seminar plans;
- mobile usability and limited collaborator views.

These are candidates for further research and low-fidelity testing, not final scope.

## Contradicting evidence and tensions retained

- **[PR]** The seminar could reportedly continue operationally without the chief organizer, but the complete picture and timely activation were also reported as highly concentrated in that person.
- **[PR]** Dependencies were described as prevented through preparation, while the workflow contained multiple managed prerequisites.
- **[PR]** No backup-plan activation was initially recognized, although the organizer substituted for a delayed instructor.
- **[PR]** Visitors judged the seminar successful while the organizer identified substantial internal weaknesses.
- **[PO]** The participant was skeptical about distributing the complete organizational playbook broadly and considered personal organizer method essential.
- **[RI]** A private cockpit may fit this participant better than a broad team workspace, but independent organizers and less experienced users must test that interpretation.

## Why the decision is REVISE

- The target niche received strong, direct support from a highly relevant participant.
- The repeatable seminar workflow received strong support.
- Organizer information concentration received strong support.
- Written ownership, completion confirmation, private contingencies, and long-term planning received support.
- The earlier broadly shared team-platform assumption conflicts with the participant's preference for private organizer control and lightweight collaborator interaction.
- More independent interviews are required.
- No prototype, observed reuse, or payment behavior was tested.

## Overall project boundary

The overall project remains `REVISE / CONTINUE VALIDATION`.

Do not begin application development, create technical architecture, or treat the organizer-cockpit hypothesis as final. Continue with independent interviews and later test a bounded low-fidelity representation before any implementation decision.

## Next research questions

1. Will independent martial-arts organizers describe the same concentration of information and activation work?
2. Would a less experienced organizer prefer a self-service playbook?
3. Would helpers actually confirm tasks through a lightweight interface?
4. How little manual input is acceptable to the chief organizer and collaborators?
5. Which contingency information must remain private?
6. Is promotion planning a core module or one checklist category?
7. Is a dependency view useful, or are guided prompts sufficient?
8. Would organizers pay for the software itself?
9. Can the system create value without the original promoter or consultant?
10. How should knowledge transfer work when the chief organizer becomes unavailable?

## Interview limitations

- The source transcript contained automatic recognition errors.
- Some questions were unclear, repeated, or leading.
- Product feedback followed concept disclosure.
- Future-success opinions are not behavioral evidence.
- One highly experienced organizer may not represent inexperienced organizers.
- The participant is personally connected to the interviewer.
- Exact confidential information was intentionally excluded.
- No prototype was tested.
- No payment commitment was tested.
