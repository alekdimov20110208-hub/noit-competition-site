# Interview INT-A2 Decision

## Decision

`REVISE`

The coordination problem remains under validation. The generic ReadyGraph concept should remain paused while a more specialized direction is tested.

## What was validated

- Small events contain real coordination dependencies.
- Missed details can cause additional work.
- The main organizer can become a single point of knowledge and responsibility.
- Reusable plans can reduce preparation work for later events.
- Reminders and clear ownership are valuable.
- Fallback information is often communicated informally.

These findings are grounded in this interview and must not yet be generalized to all organizers.

## What was not validated

- A generic platform for all event types.
- Manual what-if simulation as a strong need for small teams.
- Willingness to replace working chat and Google Drive processes.
- Task management alone as a unique product advantage.

## Main product implication

The product should not compete as another generic project-management system.

The next concept to validate should be a specialized martial-arts seminar playbook containing:

- reusable seminar templates;
- guest instructor travel and accommodation;
- venue and tatami preparation;
- registrations and payments;
- promotion;
- transport and welcoming;
- responsibilities and confirmations;
- deadlines;
- fallback people and resources;
- guided risk prompts;
- reuse of lessons and tasks from previous seminars.

AI may help create an initial draft, but the core product must work without AI and must not depend on AI for correctness.

This is a direction to validate, not a selected final pivot or an approved implementation scope.

## Comparison with PILOT-A1

Both interviews identified:

- fragmented communication;
- dependencies between people, tasks, resources, or decisions;
- late changes that require replanning;
- fallback plans communicated informally or inconsistently;
- friction caused by maintaining another platform;
- strong competition from established management tools and existing workflows.

INT-A2 added stronger evidence for:

- concrete missed details and emergency corrective work;
- the value of reusable plans and templates;
- the burden on the main organizer to remind other people;
- guided risk prompts as an alternative to relying only on manual simulations;
- the difference between introducing a tool to a new team and replacing an established process.

## Current project status

- Do not begin full implementation yet.
- Continue validation.
- Keep the generic ReadyGraph concept paused.
- Test the specialized seminar-playbook direction in later interviews and a small prototype.
- Do not treat this decision as selection of a final pivot.

## Next research questions

1. Which seminar information repeats across multiple organizers and countries?
2. Which details are most often forgotten?
3. Would organizers start from a specialized template?
4. Who would maintain the information?
5. Would reminders reduce the need for the main organizer to chase people?
6. Are guided risk questions more useful than manual simulations?
7. What unique value would make organizers use this alongside or instead of chat and Google Drive?
8. Would a new team adopt it from the start?
9. Which parts must work well on a phone?
10. Would the participant complete a 15–20 minute prototype test?

No application code or technical architecture should be created as a result of this interview decision.
