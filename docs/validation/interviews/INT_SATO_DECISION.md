# Interview INT-SATO Decision

## 1. Evidence scope and labels

This is an empty interview-level decision template. Complete it only after the consented `INT-SATO` evidence has been reviewed. Keep this decision independent from other interviews; do not perform cross-interview synthesis here.

- **[PR] Participant report** - account of a past or current process, action, incident, or consequence.
- **[PO] Participant opinion** - judgment, preference, prediction, recommendation, or future intention.
- **[CF] Contaminated concept feedback** - response after the application concept was explained or after a leading product prompt.
- **[RI] Researcher inference** - analyst interpretation that remains unvalidated until checked separately.
- **[U] Unknown** - missing, unclear, or unreliable evidence.

Contamination rules:

- Keep all responses after the neutral concept introduction labelled **[CF]**.
- Do not use **[CF]** alone as behavioral problem, solution, demand, adoption, retention, or willingness-to-pay evidence.
- Do not convert participant opinion or future intention into observed behavior.
- Do not convert one participant report into a prevalence claim.
- Keep supporting evidence, contradictory evidence, and researcher inference separate.
- Raw audio and transcripts remain outside Git; quotations require audio verification.

## 2. Hypothesis evaluated

> Host dojos organizing Aikido seminars may face recurring coordination problems involving incomplete or changing information, dependence on experienced organizers, fragmented communication, and difficulty transferring practical organizational knowledge. The international visiting-instructor perspective may reveal which problems recur across different dojos.

One interview cannot establish prevalence, demand, product-market fit, adoption, retention, willingness to pay, or authorization to build.

## 10. Contamination and interview-quality risks

- Supportive relationship.
- Participant knew an application was being researched.
- Concept discussion occurs after the behavioral section.
- Possible polite encouragement.
- Automatic transcription errors.
- Visiting-instructor perspective may not reveal all internal host-dojo work.
- No claim should rely only on future intention.

## 11. What this interview does not prove

This interview alone does not prove:

- prevalence across all Aikido dojos;
- that software is the correct solution;
- adoption;
- retention;
- replacement of existing communication;
- willingness to pay;
- commercial demand;
- success of any proposed feature;
- authorization to build the full application.
