# Interview INT-BJJ Decision

## 1. Evidence scope and labels

This is an interview-level decision based only on the approved anonymized evidence summary for `INT-BJJ`. It is independent from other interviews and does not perform cross-interview synthesis.

The raw audio and transcript remain outside Git. The approved evidence summary contains no direct observation of an organizational artifact, workflow, prototype use, purchase, payment, installation, migration, or repeat-use behavior.

- **[PR] Participant report** - account of a past or current process, action, incident, or consequence.
- **[PO] Participant opinion** - judgment, preference, prediction, recommendation, or future intention.
- **[CF] Contaminated concept feedback** - response after a product explanation or leading prompt.
- **[RI] Researcher inference** - analyst interpretation requiring further validation.
- **[U] Unknown** - missing or unreliable evidence.

## 2. Hypothesis evaluated

**Hypothesis:** Experienced martial-arts seminar organizers face recurring and consequential coordination problems because registration revenue is uncertain, event knowledge and decisions are centralized, delegated work is tracked manually, and emergency changes require rapid contextual communication. These problems may justify testing a lightweight coordination layer that complements existing communication tools.

This interview can evaluate whether the problem is credible for this participant. It cannot establish prevalence, select a market segment, or validate the proposed application.

### Decision rationale

- **[PR]** Late registration and payment create recurring uncertainty while substantial costs may already be committed.
- **[PR]** Nearly all major decisions and much of the event state remain with one experienced organizer.
- **[PR]** Delegation and completion tracking rely on verbal explanation, Viber messages, and calls.
- **[PR]** Multiple real emergency examples required rapid replanning, direct contact, and trusted human execution.
- **[PR]** Task context, dependencies, urgency, and consequences are important to successful delegation.
- **[PR]** The same informal process still works well enough at the participant's current event scale.
- **[U]** No dedicated application was adopted, retained, paid for, or used in a real event.
- **[CF]** The positive reaction to the explained application is contaminated concept feedback and cannot raise the demand assessment.

The interview strengthens the case that a real organizational problem exists. It does not show that a dedicated application is the correct or proportionate solution.

## 10. Contamination and interview-quality risks

- The transcript was generated automatically and contains recognition errors.
- Exact wording, proper names, dates, numbers, event details, and quotations require audio verification.
- Some questions were leading.
- The proposed application was explained near the end of the interview.
- Product-positive answers after that explanation are **[CF]** and may reflect framing, politeness, or a supportive relationship with the interviewer.
- The approved evidence summary contains no direct artifact or behavioral observation.
- No prototype comparison was performed against the current Viber-and-call workflow.
- No willingness-to-pay, purchase, adoption, migration, or retention behavior was tested.
- Consent details and interview duration are not established in the approved evidence summary.
- One experienced organizer may understate the burden experienced by a larger or less experienced team, but that is an **[RI]** hypothesis, not a finding.

## 11. What this interview does not prove

INT-BJJ does not prove:

- that the problem is prevalent across BJJ, martial arts, or event organizers;
- that a dedicated application is the correct solution;
- that the participant would install, adopt, retain, or pay for the application;
- that Viber, calls, or verbal communication should be replaced;
- that a system would reduce missed work, time, costs, or event risk;
- that centralization has caused an event failure;
- that reminders, dependencies, acknowledgement, escalation, or plan-B activation are validated requirements;
- that organizers will keep a shared event state current;
- that late registration/payment and task coordination belong in one product scope;
- that larger or less experienced teams experience greater pain;
- that a full product build should begin.

## 12. Product implications - hypotheses only

The following are implications to validate, not approved features or requirements:

| Hypothesis | Evidence connection | Required validation |
|---|---|---|
| A coordination layer should coexist with Viber and calls | Current channels are familiar and urgent calls remain necessary | Test a bounded workflow without forced channel replacement |
| Tasks may need rationale, deadline, dependencies, urgency, and consequence | **[PR]** The participant explicitly values this context | Compare contextual and minimal task formats in a low-fidelity test |
| Acknowledgement may matter more than notification delivery | Urgent information is handled by direct call | Test acknowledgement and escalation behavior |
| Reminders may progress from normal to warning to urgent | **[CF]** Suggested after product explanation | Test timing, comprehension, annoyance, and action rates |
| Critical overdue tasks may trigger human escalation | **[PR]/[CF]** Calls are used for consequential information | Test escalation thresholds and false-alarm burden |
| Plan-B workflows may need an explicit responsible person and current event information | Real emergency examples required rapid replacement and updates | Test one luggage or venue-change scenario without code |
| Reusable seminar templates may reduce setup effort | The process is repeatable and adjusted per event | Observe whether organizers can create and reuse a useful template quickly |
| Larger or less experienced teams may be a stronger segment | **[CF]/[RI]** Current scale is manageable for this experienced organizer | Conduct independent interviews across scale and experience levels |
| Registration and payment uncertainty may be a separate problem direction | Strongest recurring problem concerns revenue uncertainty | Validate independently before adding financial scope |

These hypotheses do not authorize feature selection, architecture, data modeling, or application code.

## 13. Next validation actions

1. Complete the Sato Sensei interview and analyze it independently.
2. Test whether late registration and late payment recur across other seminar organizers, including their actual consequences and current controls.
3. Compare organizers with larger or less experienced teams against this experienced participant's manageable current process.
4. Investigate whether organizers would use a lightweight coordination layer while continuing to use existing messaging and telephone channels.
5. Validate acknowledgement, task context, dependencies, progressive reminders, emergency escalation, and plan-B workflows through bounded low-fidelity scenarios.
6. Measure setup and update burden so that a proposed coordination layer does not create more administration than it removes.
7. Record a real adoption or purchase process before making any demand or willingness-to-pay claim.
8. Keep the positive response to the explained product classified as contaminated concept feedback.
9. Do not synthesize across interviews until the remaining independent interview has its own notes and decision.
10. Do not begin full prototype or product development before independent evidence has been synthesized and the repository's approval gates are satisfied.
