## 6. Candidate A assessment

### Supporting evidence

- **[OD]** Reusable date-relative task templates, assignments, deadlines, reminders, completion, itineraries, vendors, budgets, files, and activity records support long-range complex-event planning. See PP-S3 through PP-S5.
- **[OD]** Configurable account groups, tool permissions, view-only access, and client portals support organizer control and selective collaboration. See PP-S7.
- **[OD]** Registration, check-in, forms, transportation/lodging questions, event communications, promotion pages, and cloud-file links cover several complex seminar-adjacent jobs. See PP-S6, PP-S8, PP-S10, and PP-S14 through PP-S16.
- **[OD]** Feedback, lessons learned, final notes, archives, duplication, and reports support knowledge retention across events. See PP-S5, PP-S10, and PP-S11.

### Contradicting evidence

- **[OS]** Current positioning emphasizes venues, hospitality operations, bookings, CRM, contracts, invoicing, catering, and professional event businesses. See PP-S1.
- **[OD]** No martial-arts playbook, instructor-specific travel/accommodation, private contingency system, or explicit preparation-task dependencies were verified. See PP-S3 through PP-S16.
- **[PO]** Candidate A research warns that mature organizers may reject heavy administration or a replacement for phone/chat/Drive. See PP-R3 and PP-R4.
- **[RI]** Opaque current pricing and broad setup scope may be disproportionate for an independent volunteer-led seminar. See PP-S1, PP-S4, PP-S12, and PP-S13.

### Unknowns

- Actual setup and training effort for a small martial-arts organization.
- Whether private contingency notes can be separated safely from broad reporting access.
- Whether helper confirmation is sufficiently lightweight for volunteers.
- Current price and package containing all relevant event-planning tools.
- Whether task dependencies can represent complex guest/logistics prerequisites.

### Researcher inference

**[RI]** Planning Pod is the strongest general-event operations comparator for Candidate A in this batch. It covers most generic structures, so any remaining value must be domain-specific and lower-friction rather than “all event information in one place.” See PP-S1 through PP-S16, PP-R2, and PP-R3.

**Final relevance: `High`.**

## 7. Candidate B assessment

### Supporting evidence

- **[OD]** Reusable templates, event duplication, date-relative task lists, setup guides, registration guides, reminders, and completed-task actions reduce blank-page work. See PP-S3 through PP-S7.
- **[OD]** Paid/free registration, event webpages, check-in, forms, and attendee questions provide practical event-operation capabilities beyond a checklist. See PP-S5, PP-S6, PP-S10, and PP-S16.
- **[OP]** A 14-day no-card trial permits evaluation without immediate payment information, although no trial was entered here. See PP-S12.

### Contradicting evidence

- **[OS]** Product breadth and vocabulary center professional event/venue businesses, not an occasional martial-arts volunteer organizer. See PP-S1 and PP-S9.
- **[OD]** Creating templates may require copying/pasting and initial setup, and helper/client collaboration normally involves an invitation and login. See PP-S4 and PP-S7.
- **[OD]** Continued use requires a paid subscription; no permanent free plan is offered in the account guide, and current numeric cost is unknown. See PP-S12.
- **[PO]**, **[RI]** Candidate B requires explanations of what to do and why plus very low helper effort; generic templates and extensive tool choice do not prove that guidance. See PP-R2 and PP-R4.

### Unknowns

- Whether a first-time organizer can configure only the needed tools without overload.
- Which plan contains the relevant templates, registration, permissions, and forms.
- Whether current cost is acceptable for an infrequent seminar.
- Whether pre-built templates explain rationale or merely provide generic task lists.
- Whether volunteers would accept account creation and portal use.

### Researcher inference

**[RI]** Planning Pod can technically support a first seminar, but its breadth and paid professional model may create more setup than Candidate B needs. It remains a strong baseline against which a specialized guided playbook must prove lower effort and clearer martial-arts adaptation. See PP-S1 through PP-S16, PP-R2, and PP-R4.

**Final relevance: `Medium`.**

## 9. Differentiation implications

### Capabilities already covered

- **[OD]** Reusable templates, event duplication, task ownership, deadlines, reminders, completion, permissions, files, registration, check-in, vendors, budgets, forms, mobile web, feedback, and archives. See PP-S3 through PP-S16.
- **[OS]** Broad event/venue workflows, communications, automation, promotion surfaces, and integrations. See PP-S1 and PP-S2.

### Capabilities partially covered

- Seminar registration, travel/lodging data, promotion, merchandise-related attendee data, dependency-like workflows, restricted access, and onboarding exist in general-purpose forms but are not martial-arts seminar playbooks. See PP-S3 through PP-S16.

### Credible remaining gaps

- **[RI]** Martial-arts-specific explanations, instructor protocol/logistics, context-aware private contingencies, low-administration helper confirmation, and a deliberately small first-seminar path remain credible. See PP-R2 through PP-R4 and PP-S1 through PP-S16.
- **[RI]** A workflow designed to link existing chat/Drive resources without requiring a full venue/CRM/business stack remains credible. See PP-S8, PP-S14, and PP-R4.

### Gaps that are only unknown, not proven

- Explicit task dependencies, exact plan boundaries, current cost, small-team onboarding time, martial-arts customer use, and no-account helper confirmation are `Unknown` rather than absent.

### Features not to copy merely because Planning Pod has them

- **[RI]** CRM pipelines, contracts, invoices, catering, BEOs, floorplans, venue booking, client sales portals, and dozens of configurable tools should not enter a seminar product without validated need. See PP-S1 and PP-R2.

No implementation recommendation is made.

## 10. Classification review

`KEEP AS ADJACENT`

**[RI]** Planning Pod verifies substantial generic event-planning overlap and is the strongest Candidate A comparator in this batch, but it does not specialize in martial arts or provide a verified seminar-specific playbook, instructor logistics model, or private contingency method. It therefore remains `ADJACENT`, not `DIRECT`; its breadth prevents justified deprioritization. See PP-S1 through PP-S16, PP-R1, and PP-R2.
