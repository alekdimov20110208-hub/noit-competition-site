## 6. Candidate A assessment

### Supporting evidence

- **[OS]** The article covers goals/audience/metrics, projected and actual budget, venue, layout/branding, catering, entertainment/experience, promotion, vendors, contingencies, a detailed event schedule, day-of checks, and post-event review. See EC-S1.
- **[OS]** Contingency examples include technical failure, speaker cancellation, and weather, while post-event guidance includes feedback, team review, metrics, budget/ROI, and testimonials. See EC-S1.
- **[RI]** This breadth makes the article a useful completeness prompt or review aid even for an experienced organizer. See EC-R3.

### Contradicting or limiting evidence

- **[OS]** No persistent state, private layer, owners, reminders, confirmations, dependencies, files, messaging, or change propagation exists. See EC-S1.
- **[OS]** The schedule example is event-day oriented; no long-range seminar countdown or instructor travel/accommodation workflow is supplied. See EC-S1.
- **[RI]** Candidate A's value hypothesis centers on preserving complex organizer logic and knowledge over time, which a public article cannot do. See EC-R2 and EC-R3.
- **[PR]** Experienced organizers already use memory, personal routines, calls, paper, chat, and private materials; reading a generic list may add little unless it surfaces a missed category. See EC-R3.

### Important unknowns

- Whether experienced martial-arts organizers would use the article as a review aid and whether it reveals real omissions.
- How much adaptation is required to translate general categories into a recurring martial-arts seminar.
- Whether linked separate templates provide a more usable package than the article alone.
- Whether teams preserve completed adaptations for the next event or return to memory/chat.

### Researcher inference

**[RI]** The article has medium Candidate A relevance as a broad audit list, especially for contingency and post-event review, but cannot displace a mature team's operational or private planning system. See EC-S1, EC-R2, and EC-R3.

**Final Candidate A relevance: `Medium`.**

- Which of the 13 parts prevent actual omissions versus add reading/setup work.

## 9. Differentiation implications

### Capabilities already covered

- **[OS]** Free, public, ungated guidance across goals, budget, venue, design, food, experience, promotion, vendors, contingencies, schedule, day-of execution, and post-event review. See EC-S1.
- **[OS]** Concrete prompts/examples and links to deeper resources provide a credible general-event `what and why` baseline. See EC-S1.

### Capabilities partially covered

- **[OS]** Delegation, timing, contingencies, merchandise, speakers, and learning are discussed but not represented as structured state. See EC-S1.
- **[RI]** The ordered article approximates a reusable playbook at the information layer, but not at the adaptation, tracking, confirmation, or retention layers. See EC-S1 and EC-R2.

### Credible remaining gaps

- **[RI]** Martial-arts seminar-specific prioritization, timing, instructor logistics, role expectations, and optional paths. See EC-R2 through EC-R4.
- **[RI]** Lightweight conversion of guidance into owners, deadlines, confirmations, private contingencies, and retained lessons without imposing heavy helper administration. See EC-R3 and EC-R4.
- **[RI]** Contextual reuse that shows what changed from the last seminar and why. See EC-R2 through EC-R4.

### Unknowns that are not proven gaps

- Target readers' comprehension, completion, trust, search discovery, and real omission-prevention outcomes.
- Format and access conditions for every linked template.
- Whether a target organizer already has a superior personal checklist.

### Features that should not be copied merely because the article includes them

- **[RI]** Every general-event topic need not become a product module. Entertainment, influencer relations, catering, ticket-profit metrics, and testimonials may be optional or irrelevant for some seminars. See EC-S1 and EC-R2.
- **[RI]** Breadth should be treated as a coverage benchmark, not as validated scope or permission to implement. See EC-R2 through EC-R4.

## 12. Profile conclusion

**[RI]** `An Event Planning Checklist for Any Event` is a strong informational substitute: free, public, broad, explanatory, and unusually complete for a single article. Its strongest overlap is Candidate B's guided `what and why` layer, and its most important limitation is the complete absence of operational state or domain adaptation. It therefore validates that the playbook hypothesis faces a credible free baseline, not that the hypothesis has user-demand validation. The profile keeps `INFORMATIONAL SUBSTITUTE`, Candidate A `Medium`, and Candidate B `High`; it selects no final segment and authorizes no prototype or development work. See EC-S1 and EC-R1 through EC-R4.
