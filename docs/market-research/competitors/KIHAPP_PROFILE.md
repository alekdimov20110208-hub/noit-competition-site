## 6. Candidate A assessment

### Supporting evidence

- **[OS]** Kihapp covers martial-arts-specific registration, fees, divisions, brackets, schedules, scoring, and results, reducing operational load for events that include competition. See KH-S1.
- **[OD]** Owner, admin, observer, and designated operational roles support differentiated access, including link/QR access for some authorized users. See KH-S4.
- **[DO]** Current public pages show combined tournament/seminar packages, schedules, documents, and organizer information in one event surface. See KH-S5.
- **[OS]** Web access across desktop and mobile platforms and PDF brackets offer practical event-day reach and a limited connectivity fallback. See KH-S1 and KH-S2.

### Contradicting evidence

- **[OS]** Product messaging and documented logic center tournaments, competition categories, draws, scoring, and results. See KH-S1 and KH-S2.
- **[DO]** The seminar examples show registration/ticket presentation, not long-range planning, guest logistics, private contingencies, promotion/merchandise checkpoints, or lessons learned. See KH-S5 and KH-S6.
- **[PR]**, **[PO]** Candidate A's reported value includes private organizer control, instructor travel, hidden fallbacks, and knowledge transfer, none of which is established by the Kihapp sources. See KH-R3.

### Unknowns

- Whether Kihapp provides a separate organizer workflow for seminar-only events.
- Whether event duplication or reusable event templates exist.
- Whether private notes or contingency data can be limited to a trusted subset.
- Whether general helper assignments and confirmations exist outside tournament roles.
- Whether promotion, merchandise, and post-event review have organizer tools.

### Researcher inference

**[RI]** Kihapp may be a strong operational companion for a complex martial-arts event with registration or competition, but it does not publicly establish the chief organizer's preparation cockpit. See KH-S1 through KH-S6, KH-R2, and KH-R3.

**Final relevance: `Medium`.**

## 9. Differentiation implications

### Capabilities already covered

- **[OS]** Martial-arts registration, payment tracking, division configuration, brackets, schedules, scoring, scoreboards, results, and browser-based operation. See KH-S1 and KH-S2.
- **[OD]** Organizer and operational access roles, including limited no-account link/QR access for designated roles. See KH-S4.
- **[DO]** Public seminar ticket/division presentation and training-camp registration use. See KH-S5 and KH-S6.

### Capabilities partially covered

- Seminar/event presentation, public event documents, email confirmations, registration deadlines, organizer permissions, and mobile browser use are covered in limited or tournament-centered ways. The public-page evidence does not establish organizer document management or a travel-document workflow. See KH-S2, KH-S4, KH-S5, and KH-S7.

### Credible remaining gaps

- **[RI]** Reusable seminar playbooks, long-range organizer tasks, private contingency logic, instructor travel/accommodation, promotion and merchandise checkpoints, and post-event learning remain credible specialized gaps. See KH-R2 through KH-R4 and KH-S1 through KH-S8.
- **[RI]** General-helper ownership and one-tap confirmation outside competition roles remain credible but unvalidated. See KH-S4 and KH-R4.

### Gaps that are only unknown, not proven

- Whole-event templates, cloud-file integrations, organizer notes, task reminders, seminar-only pricing, and a distinct seminar workflow are `Unknown`; public non-discovery does not prove absence.

### Features not to copy merely because Kihapp has them

- **[RI]** Bracket algorithms, scoring devices, livestream graphics, weigh-ins, and competition-result publishing should not enter a seminar-planning scope without validated need. See KH-S1 and KH-R2.

No implementation recommendation is made.

## 10. Classification review

`KEEP AS ADJACENT`

**[RI]** Kihapp is martial-arts-specific and demonstrably supports seminar registration within public event surfaces, but its verified primary job remains tournament/event registration and execution. No current evidence establishes substantial overlap with the long-range planning, guided playbook, private contingencies, helper ownership, knowledge transfer, or post-event learning required for `DIRECT`. See KH-S1 through KH-S8, KH-R1, and KH-R2.

## 11. Confidence and evidence gaps

- **Overall confidence: `Medium`.**
- **Strongest verified findings:** **[OS]** martial-arts specialization, registration, payments, divisions, brackets, schedules, scoring, results, and browser support; **[OP]** percentage pricing; **[OD]** role model; **[DO]** public seminar/training-camp use. See KH-S1 through KH-S8.
- **Important unknowns:** seminar-only organizer flow, whole-event templates, task/deadline management, contingencies, structured instructor logistics, cloud/chat integrations, merchandise planning, post-event learning, and free-event pricing.
- **Inaccessible information:** authenticated organizer setup beyond exposed demo/configuration pages, actual support experience, and complete plan/limit documentation.
- **Would direct observation materially change the conclusion?** Yes. An approved future organizer demonstration could determine whether seminars use a distinct workflow, how reusable events are, what permissions expose, and whether general planning tasks exist. It would not by itself prove segment demand.

## 12. Profile conclusion

**[RI]** Kihapp is the closest martial-arts-specific product in this batch, but verified overlap is concentrated in registration and tournament/event execution. Public evidence shows seminars can be represented and sold within Kihapp, not that Kihapp provides a complete seminar-planning playbook or chief-organizer cockpit. The profile therefore preserves `KEEP AS ADJACENT`, rates both Candidate A and Candidate B relevance `Medium`, does not select a segment, and does not authorize a prototype.
