## 6. Candidate A assessment

### Supporting evidence

- **[PR]** One mature recurring seminar team reported that messaging and Google Drive worked well, which is direct participant-level evidence of adequacy for an incumbent workflow, not a general market statistic. See MG-R4.
- **[OD]** Drive roles, limited-access folders, versions, offline files, and optional shared-drive governance can hold a long-range/private body of organizer information. See MG-S2 through MG-S8.
- **[OS]** Messaging groups add rapid updates, replies, reactions, polls, files, calls, events/reminders, and admin controls with tool-specific differences. See MG-S11 through MG-S18.
- **[RI]** Arbitrary Docs/Sheets/Forms/Calendar structures can represent instructor itineraries, budgets, promotion, merchandise, contingency plans, and post-event learning without waiting for product-specific modules. See MG-R2 and MG-R3.

### Contradicting or limiting evidence

- **[RI]** Flexibility is purchased with manual design: the organizer must establish folder hierarchy, source-of-truth rules, ownership columns, deadlines, status meanings, permission boundaries, and reuse conventions. See MG-S2 through MG-S8 and MG-R2.
- **[OD]** Strong native Docs task assignment is limited to work/school accounts and same-organization assignees; consumer helpers may need manual tables, comments, or chat acknowledgements. See MG-S6.
- **[OD]** Folder permissions inherit, editors can move/delete files, and anyone-link sharing trades access friction for link-control risk. See MG-S2 and MG-S3.
- **[RI]** Chat can fragment decisions, duplicate files, mix urgency with history, and create notification noise; no unified record reconciles a message reaction with a Drive task and deadline. See MG-S11 through MG-S18 and MG-R2.

### Important unknowns

- The exact structure, naming, access, backup, and source-of-truth conventions used by successful seminar teams.
- How often chat decisions fail to reach the durable plan or Drive files become stale/duplicated.
- Whether helpers prefer link/comment/reaction flows and what level of confirmation is reliable without paid Workspace accounts.
- Bulgarian prevalence of Google Drive, WhatsApp, Viber, and Messenger in the target population.
- Offline/weak-connectivity behavior in actual venues and conflict recovery across devices.
- Whether mature teams perceive any unmet job large enough to justify another layer.

### Researcher inference

**[RI]** This remains a high-relevance Candidate A substitute because it already covers the full range of information types and urgent communication with familiar tools, and one relevant team reported success. Its vulnerability is not missing raw flexibility; it is manual coherence, private/operational linkage, reliable confirmation, and transferable seminar knowledge. See MG-S1 through MG-S18 and MG-R2 through MG-R4.

**Final Candidate A relevance: `High`.**

## 7. Candidate B assessment

### Supporting evidence

- **[OP]** Existing accounts plus the free 15 GB Google tier can provide a low-cost starting point. See MG-S1.
- **[OD]** Anyone-link viewing, familiar comments/editing, offline files, version history, and mobile access reduce infrastructure barriers. See MG-S2 through MG-S7.
- **[OS]** Messaging tools give familiar replies, reactions, polls, events/reminders, and calls that can support lightweight helper participation. See MG-S11 through MG-S18.
- **[RI]** A competent organizer can share a single checklist/document and use chat for questions, creating a minimal alternative to a dedicated playbook. See MG-R2 and MG-R4.

### Contradicting or limiting evidence

- **[OS]** No tool supplies martial-arts seminar `what and why`; the novice must create or find the content and decide structure, timing, owners, privacy, and reuse. See MG-S1 through MG-S18.
- **[OD]** Native assigned tasks are constrained to work/school domain users, while public/anonymous access cannot provide equivalent task ownership. See MG-S4 and MG-S6.
- **[RI]** First-time teams may be especially vulnerable to scattered links, inconsistent folder permissions, ambiguous status, missing dependencies, and chat-only decisions. See MG-S2 through MG-S8 and MG-R2.
- **[PR]** The positive interview evidence comes from a mature team with a learned routine; it cannot be generalized to first-time organizers. See MG-R4.

### Important unknowns

- Whether Candidate B organizers can assemble an adequate Drive/chat workflow without expert help.
- Which seminar guidance/template they already find online or receive from instructors/associations.
- Setup time, omission rate, ownership clarity, and helper response reliability in a real novice team.
- Whether a dedicated playbook adds enough value to overcome another link/app/process.
- Which sharing model balances no-account access and private information safely.

### Researcher inference

**[RI]** The composite has high Candidate B relevance because it is the simplest host for a manually created or downloaded playbook and uses familiar helper channels. Its lack of built-in seminar guidance leaves the Candidate B content hypothesis open, but raises a demanding substitution test: any new workflow must add domain structure while remaining as easy to share and acknowledge as a document link plus chat. See MG-R1, MG-R2, and MG-R4.

**Final Candidate B relevance: `High`.**

## 9. Differentiation implications

### Capabilities already covered

- **[OD]** Shared files/folders, viewer/commenter/editor roles, restricted/link access, permission inheritance, version history, copies/restores, offline files, and optional organization-owned shared drives. See MG-S1 through MG-S8.
- **[OS]** Familiar private/group communication with replies, reactions, forwarding, media/files, calls, polls, events/reminders, admin controls, and tool-specific encryption/privacy. See MG-S11 through MG-S18.
- **[PR]** Demonstrated adequacy for at least one mature recurring team and low helper appetite for additional administration. See MG-R4.

### Capabilities partially covered

- **[OD]** Ownership, deadlines, and completion exist strongly only in specific Workspace/task contexts and otherwise rely on document/chat conventions. See MG-S6.
- **[RI]** Private contingencies, instructor logistics, promotion, merchandise, and learning can all be represented, but the system supplies no schema, prompts, or completeness guarantee. See MG-S2 through MG-S8 and MG-R3.
- **[RI]** Reuse and knowledge transfer are technically available through copies/history/shared drives, but depend on consistent recording and governance. See MG-S5, MG-S8, MG-R3, and MG-R4.

### Credible remaining gaps

- **[RI]** A seminar-specific source of truth linking guidance, organizer-only reasoning, owners, deadlines, confirmations, dependencies, and retained lessons without duplicating Drive/chat. See MG-R2 through MG-R4.
- **[RI]** Low-burden helper views/confirmations that work across account boundaries and preserve a durable organizer record. See MG-S2 through MG-S6 and MG-R4.
- **[RI]** Reliable change propagation from an urgent chat decision into affected tasks/files/people. See MG-S11 through MG-S18 and MG-R3.

### Unknowns that are not proven gaps

- Real target configurations, failure frequency, setup/maintenance effort, Bulgarian adoption, offline performance, and satisfaction.
- Whether a disciplined folder/template already closes most gaps for Candidate A or B.
- Whether integrations/automations outside this bounded source set materially reduce fragmentation.

### Features that should not be copied merely because the tools have them

- **[RI]** Large communities, broadcast channels, stickers, AI features, enterprise Vault/DLP, endpoint management, and broad office-suite functionality are not validated seminar requirements. See MG-S9 and MG-S11 through MG-S18.
- **[RI]** A new concept should learn from link access, familiar chat, versions, and flexible files without attempting to reproduce Drive or a messaging network. See MG-R2 through MG-R4.

## 10. Classification review

**Classification result: `KEEP AS SUBSTITUTE WORKFLOW`.**

**[RI]** The composite is not a direct or adjacent seminar product, but it can already perform much of the practical job through flexible files, sharing, versions, offline access, familiar group communication, and manual conventions. Official evidence and the mature-team report make it too important to deprioritize. See MG-S1 through MG-S18, MG-R1, and MG-R4.

## 11. Confidence and evidence gaps

- **Overall confidence:** **[RI]** High for official Drive storage/sharing/version/offline/task/governance/pricing and the cited messaging features; Medium for composite performance, cost, adoption, and target fit. See MG-S1 through MG-S18.
- **Strongest verified finding:** **[PR]** A mature recurring team reported that messaging plus Drive worked well; official sources explain why the stack can be capable, but the participant report must not be generalized beyond that case. See MG-R4.
- **Most important unknown:** The real amount and consequence of manual coordination work: how often source-of-truth drift, missed updates, permission mistakes, unclear ownership, and uncaptured knowledge occur in Candidate A and Candidate B teams.
- **Tool-specific limits:** WhatsApp's official 2022 announcement recorded groups up to 1,024 and 2 GB files, while Viber's current group help says 250 and distinguishes communities/channels; a current Messenger group-participant limit was not established. These limits must not be merged or assumed stable. See MG-S11, MG-S14, MG-S15, and MG-S18.
- **Inaccessible information:** Live account policies, actual folder/chat structures, current app interfaces, search/history completeness, export/backup behavior, notification load, mobile/offline conflicts, and Bulgarian account/billing availability were not accessed.
- **Would a future workflow study materially change the conclusion?** Yes. An approved observation of anonymized real preparation artifacts and coordination episodes could quantify setup/maintenance, failure modes, switching cost, and helper friction. It must preserve privacy and cannot fabricate results.
- **Competition-year boundary:** **[RI]** The mandatory NOIT regulations reviewed are for 2025-2026. Dates, submission mechanics, category wording, and other year-specific rules require verification for a later competition year; this documentation-only profile makes no implementation decision from stale rules. See the repository regulations and MG-R2.

## 12. Profile conclusion

**[RI]** Messaging plus Google Drive remains a strong substitute workflow for both candidates. Its strongest overlap is flexible, familiar, low-incremental-cost coordination: private/shared files, arbitrary plans, versions, offline access, chat, polls, replies, events/reminders, and calls can cover nearly any seminar information type. Its most important weakness is not lack of raw capability but the organizer labor required to create and maintain coherence across tools. The interview evidence shows success for one mature team, not universal adequacy or demand. The profile keeps `SUBSTITUTE WORKFLOW`, Candidate A `High`, and Candidate B `High`; it selects no final segment and authorizes no prototype or development work. See MG-S1 through MG-S18 and MG-R1 through MG-R4.
