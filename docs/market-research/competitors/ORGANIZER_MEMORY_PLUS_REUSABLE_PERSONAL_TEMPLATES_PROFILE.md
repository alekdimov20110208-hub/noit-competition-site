# Organizer Memory Plus Reusable Personal Templates Substitute-Workflow Profile

## 1. Status and scope

| Field | Status |
|---|---|
| Product or workflow | Organizer memory plus reusable personal templates: a composite substitute workflow combining tacit, person-dependent knowledge with external artifacts such as copied spreadsheets, documents, paper checklists, private notes, earlier budgets, schedules, promotion materials, instructor itineraries, and folder structures. The mix varies by organizer; memory and templates are not treated as identical. |
| Current inventory classification | `SUBSTITUTE WORKFLOW` - **[RI]** The inventory records organizer memory and reusable personal templates as separate high-priority substitutes and later shortlists their combination for detailed review. See OM-R1. |
| Research date | 2026-07-20 |
| Detailed-profile status | Complete for batch 4 repository-evidence review. The exact artifacts, copying practices, update discipline, access rules, setup effort, and outcomes across independent seminar teams remain unverified. |

## 6. Candidate A assessment

### Supporting evidence

- **[PR]** A chief organizer described stable reference points and a long preparation sequence covering approval, invitation, confirmation, announcement, travel, promotion, logistics, and schedule work. See OM-R4.
- **[PR]** The same case concentrated most information, decisions, timely activation, and contingencies in the chief organizer while distributing execution among trusted helpers. See OM-R4.
- **[PR]** A mature independent team learned its recurring routine well enough to discontinue an earlier Excel responsibility plan, while the chief instructor retained the complete picture. See OM-R5.
- **[PO]** Personal control and restricted sharing of the full organizer method were preferred, and heavy helper administration or replacement of calls/chat/Drive was resisted. See OM-R4 and OM-R5.

### Contradicting or limiting evidence

- **[PR]** One mature team reported that messaging and Drive worked well, so memory/templates are often part of a broader tool workflow rather than a complete standalone replacement. See OM-R5 and OM-R6.
- **[PR]** Oral assignment, trust-based completion, and memory-held contingencies supported execution in the complex case, but they did not provide a systematic transfer or confirmation record. See OM-R4.
- **[PO]** An experienced organizer in a stable routine was predicted not to use another planning application routinely, raising the switching threshold for any proposed change. See OM-R5.
- **[RI]** Staff turnover and organizer unavailability create a potentially material transfer question, but no observed handoff or replacement event establishes the actual consequence. See OM-R3 through OM-R5.

### Important unknowns

- **[RI]** The exact artifacts that experienced organizers keep, how often they are copied, and which contain reasons rather than only outputs are unknown. See OM-R3 through OM-R5.
- **[RI]** Template freshness, missed-task visibility, successor comprehension, and the effort needed to transfer instructor-logistics knowledge have not been directly tested. See OM-R2 through OM-R5.
- **[RI]** No evidence establishes prevalence, willingness to switch, willingness to pay, or a common threshold at which complexity outweighs familiar habits. See OM-R2 through OM-R5.

### Researcher inference

**[RI]** For Candidate A, tacit knowledge can be highly effective because it compresses years of local context into fast decisions and selective delegation. That same compression raises the transfer burden: another person may receive a schedule or checklist without the organizer's rationale, priority rules, relationship knowledge, or contingency triggers. This is the strongest currently evidenced Candidate A substitute because it is embedded in the organizer's method and can absorb any supporting tool; messaging plus Drive is its strongest complementary tool workflow. See OM-R1 and OM-R3 through OM-R6.

**[RI] Final Candidate A relevance: `High`.** See OM-R1 and OM-R3 through OM-R6.

## 7. Candidate B assessment

### Supporting evidence

- **[PR]** Another martial-arts group sought experienced organizational advice, supporting a transfer channel from an experienced organizer to a less-prepared group. See OM-R3 and OM-R4.
- **[PR]** Repeated stages, earlier responsibility sheets, organized folders, paper records, and prior materials can provide a starting structure that a novice would otherwise have to create. See OM-R3 through OM-R6.
- **[RI]** Reusing an existing artifact can be low-cost and easier to adapt than configuring a broad event or task-management product from a blank workspace. See OM-R6 through OM-R8.

### Contradicting or limiting evidence

- **[RI]** A first-time organizer does not possess the memory half of the composite and may receive no personal template at all. See OM-R2 through OM-R5.
- **[PR]** The complete picture remained with experienced leaders even where helpers knew recurring operational work, indicating that an artifact can omit strategic context. See OM-R3 and OM-R5.
- **[RI]** A copied template may show prior outputs without explaining what is mandatory, optional, context-dependent, or why a task exists; no novice used or interpreted one in the evidence set. See OM-R3 through OM-R5 and OM-R7.
- **[RI]** Free public guidance and generic task templates already offer other low-cost starting points, so personal templates are not automatically the simplest or most complete option. See OM-R7 and OM-R8.

### Important unknowns

- **[RI]** Whether Candidate B can access a trusted organizer's materials, whether mentoring accompanies them, and whether permission/privacy concerns restrict sharing are unknown. See OM-R3 through OM-R5.
- **[RI]** Ease of adaptation, omission prevention, setup time, helper delegation, confirmation quality, and martial-arts-specific comprehension have not been observed with a first-time organizer. See OM-R2 through OM-R5.
- **[RI]** No evidence establishes whether Candidate B expects free guidance, pays for help, or prefers a personal template over a public checklist or generic task template. See OM-R2, OM-R5, OM-R7, and OM-R8.

### Researcher inference

**[RI]** Candidate B can benefit from the template portion only when artifacts and explanation are available. The memory portion remains with the experienced person, so dependence may shift from self-recall to mentoring rather than disappear. This makes the composite relevant but not yet a validated self-service substitute for first-time organizers. See OM-R3 through OM-R5 and OM-R7.

**[RI] Final Candidate B relevance: `Medium`.** See OM-R3 through OM-R5 and OM-R7.

## 9. Differentiation implications

### Capabilities already covered by the incumbent workflow

- **[RI]** Familiar planning sequences, rapid expert action, arbitrary customization, private control, selective sharing, remembered contingencies, and reuse of earlier artifacts. See OM-R3 through OM-R5.
- **[RI]** Compatibility with phone, chat, paper, spreadsheets, documents, cloud folders, and face-to-face coordination without one mandatory platform. See OM-R3 through OM-R6.
- **[RI]** Potentially low incremental monetary cost and low helper-administration burden when existing habits and tools are retained. See OM-R3 through OM-R6.

### Capabilities only partially covered

- **[PR]** Ownership, deadlines, completion, dependencies, instructor logistics, promotion, merchandise, and contingencies can be handled, but their written visibility and consistency varied in the reviewed cases. See OM-R3 through OM-R5.
- **[RI]** Post-event learning can update memory or artifacts, but a durable change record connecting lessons to the next template was not established. See OM-R3 through OM-R5.
- **[RI]** Sharing can preserve privacy and reduce helper burden, but it can also leave rationale and broader contingency knowledge unavailable to a successor. See OM-R3 through OM-R6.

### Credible research gaps

- **[RI]** Whether a successor can understand task purpose, priority, dependencies, and activation conditions from an experienced organizer's artifacts without prolonged mentoring. See OM-R3 through OM-R5.
- **[RI]** Whether old artifacts contain stale assumptions and whether teams have a dependable review/update practice. No frequency or failure impact is claimed. See OM-R2 through OM-R5.
- **[RI]** Whether lightweight structure can preserve the incumbent's speed, privacy, and flexibility while making only selected knowledge transferable. See OM-R2 through OM-R6.

### Switching and adoption implications

- **[PO]** Adoption resistance is strongest when another system duplicates chat/Drive updates, requires helper accounts, exposes private know-how, or adds ongoing status administration. See OM-R3 through OM-R6.
- **[RI]** Migration burden is highest for tacit rationale and relationship knowledge, not necessarily for copying concrete files. See OM-R3 through OM-R6.
- **[RI]** A future concept would face trust/privacy concerns, loss-of-flexibility fears, and a high switching threshold among successful experts who may not perceive an unmet problem. This remains a research inference, not a recommendation to build. See OM-R3 through OM-R6.
- **[RI]** Coexistence is more credible than replacement: personal templates can remain authoritative while any later tested concept handles only a bounded transfer, reminder, or confirmation job. This is a validation implication, not approved product scope. See OM-R2 through OM-R6.

## 10. Classification review

**[RI] Classification result: `KEEP AS SUBSTITUTE WORKFLOW`.** See OM-R1 through OM-R6.

**[RI]** The composite directly matches incumbent behavior reported for experienced seminar organization and can absorb paper, spreadsheets, documents, chat, cloud folders, and calls without becoming one product. It is not `DIRECT`, `ADJACENT`, or merely informational, and it should not be described as broken. See OM-R1 through OM-R6.

**[RI]** Within the current evidence set, organizer memory plus reusable personal templates is the strongest Candidate A substitute because the organizer's method remains the control layer even when other tools store or communicate parts of it. Messaging plus Google Drive is the strongest complementary tool workflow. This is a bounded comparative judgment, not a prevalence claim. See OM-R1 and OM-R3 through OM-R6.

**[RI]** Candidate B remains relevant through transferred templates and mentoring, but the lack of a directly studied first-time organizer and the absence of observed self-service transfer prevent a `High` rating. See OM-R3 through OM-R5 and OM-R7.

**Proposed inventory correction:** **[RI]** Preserve the separate `Reusable personal templates` and `Organizer memory` rows because they are not identical. Add a later cross-reference that they commonly form a composite substitute; if a compact summary combines them, avoid double-counting and use Candidate A `High`, Candidate B `Medium`, with the note that novices need access to artifacts and explanation. Do not edit the inventory in this task. See OM-R1 through OM-R5.

**Effect on direct-competitor result:** **[RI]** None. This workflow is not a commercial product and does not meet the repository definition of a direct competitor, so the inventory's `0 DIRECT` finding remains unchanged. See OM-R1 and OM-R2.

**Targeted follow-up implication:** **[RI]** The profile supports a focused later validation question about whether a first-time organizer or successor can use another person's template without live explanation and which private reasoning must remain with the chief organizer. It does not make the final market decision or authorize a prototype. See OM-R2 through OM-R5.

## 11. Confidence and evidence gaps

- **Overall confidence:** **[RI]** High for the described participant-level use of recurring routines, memory concentration, informal ownership/confirmation, private contingencies, and mature-team learning; Medium for the composite classification; Low-to-Medium for cross-team prevalence, transfer outcomes, staleness, cost, and switching behavior. See OM-R1 and OM-R3 through OM-R5.
- **Strongest verified strength:** **[PR]** An experienced organizer can act through stable reference points, long-range learned sequencing, trusted delegation, and private contingency knowledge, while a mature team can internalize recurring work enough to reduce formal planning overhead. See OM-R4 and OM-R5.
- **Most important limitation:** **[PR]** The complete picture and broader contingencies remained concentrated in senior organizers, while systematic ownership, completion confirmation, and a complete transfer record were not established. See OM-R3 through OM-R5.
- **Critical Candidate A unknown:** **[RI]** What happens to quality, timeliness, privacy, and decision-making when the chief organizer is unavailable or staff changes; no actual handoff was observed. See OM-R3 through OM-R5.
- **Critical Candidate B unknown:** **[RI]** Whether another organizer's artifacts explain enough `what`, `why`, `when`, and `who` to prevent meaningful omissions without mentoring. No first-time organizer has been interviewed or observed. See OM-R3 through OM-R5.
- **Template-quality unknown:** **[RI]** No artifact was inspected, so completeness, freshness, internal contradictions, copied stale assumptions, version control, and incorporation of post-event lessons remain unknown. See OM-R2 through OM-R5.
- **Evaluation blind spot:** **[RI]** Successful experts may experience the workflow as effortless and may not perceive a problem even when knowledge transfer remains difficult; the evidence does not establish how common or consequential that tension is. See OM-R3 through OM-R5.
- **Would later direct observation materially change the conclusion?** **[RI]** Yes. With explicit consent and privacy protection, comparing anonymized prior-event artifacts with a real preparation cycle could test reuse, freshness, rationale, missing-task visibility, and transfer. No such observation is claimed here. See OM-R2 through OM-R5.
- **Competition-year boundary:** **[RI]** The mandatory NOIT regulations reviewed apply to 2025-2026. Their dates, submission procedures, category wording, documentation formatting, and other year-specific rules must be rechecked for a later competition year; this documentation-only profile makes no implementation decision. See OM-R2.

## 12. Profile conclusion

**[RI]** Organizer memory plus reusable personal templates remains the strongest currently evidenced Candidate A substitute and a medium-relevance Candidate B substitute. Its verified strength is fit with an experienced organizer's habits: speed, personal control, private judgment, selective delegation, local customization, and reuse can coexist with any familiar tool. Its central limitation is transferability: an artifact can preserve outputs while the reasons, priorities, dependencies, relationship knowledge, and contingency triggers remain tacit. The profile keeps `SUBSTITUTE WORKFLOW`, preserves the distinction between memory and templates, changes neither the `0 DIRECT` result nor the unresolved segment decision, and authorizes no prototype or development work. See OM-R1 through OM-R7.
