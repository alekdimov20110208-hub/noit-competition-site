## 6. Candidate A assessment

### Supporting evidence

- **[OD]** The dedicated seminar guide covers one-day bookings and multi-day dated memberships, sign-up forms, waivers, programs/classes, payments, attendance, and participant tracking. See GD-S3.
- **[OD]** Staff permissions, assigned tasks, due dates, notes, and open/done status create an internal organizer layer. See GD-S7 and GD-S8.
- **[OD]** Scheduling, capacity, waitlists, email/SMS, member access, payments, QR/numeric check-in, products/stock, and reports cover substantial execution and event-day work. See GD-S4, GD-S5, and GD-S9 through GD-S19.
- **[OS]** Martial-arts specialization reduces domain mismatch relative to generic event or project tools. See GD-S1.

### Contradicting or limiting evidence

- **[OD]** The seminar workflow is assembled from schedule, membership, program, form, booking, waiver, payment, and attendance objects; no official source shows a seminar project plan or workback timeline. See GD-S3.
- **[OD]** Staff tasks are generic and documented mainly around member/lead follow-up; dependencies, milestones, and helper confirmation for arbitrary seminar preparation are not established. See GD-S8.
- **[RI]** Private contingencies, instructor travel/accommodation, structured post-event learning, and transfer of organizer reasoning remain missing evidence against Candidate A's deeper jobs. See GD-R2 and GD-R3.
- **[OP]** A full ongoing subscription starts at `$75 USD/month`, with processor and SMS costs; a mature team already using chat/Drive may face duplicate administration. See GD-S2, GD-S9, GD-S11, and GD-R4.

### Important unknowns

- Whether a seminar-only organizer can practically use Gymdesk without maintaining an ongoing member-management dataset.
- Whether staff tasks can be organized, filtered, copied, or automated into a reusable end-to-end seminar workback plan.
- Bulgarian processor onboarding, settlement currency, tax, SMS availability, and actual total cost.
- Actual mobile/browser reliability and staff usability during weak-connectivity event conditions.
- Whether integrations can preserve a mature team's Drive documents and chat without duplicate status entry.

### Researcher inference

**[RI]** Gymdesk is a stronger Candidate A comparator than the discovery summary implied because the product documents seminars explicitly and combines registrations, payments, attendance, communications, tasks, stock, and reporting. It still does not evidence the long-range private preparation system at the center of Candidate A, so the relevance remains `Medium`. See GD-S3 through GD-S19, GD-R2, and GD-R3.

**Final Candidate A relevance: `Medium`.**

## 7. Candidate B assessment

### Supporting evidence

- **[OD]** A dedicated seminar/bootcamp article gives concrete product-configuration steps for one-day and multi-day formats. See GD-S3.
- **[OD]** Sign-up forms, waivers, bookings, capacity, waitlists, payments, confirmations/reminders, and member access package many common administrative needs. See GD-S3 through GD-S6 and GD-S9 through GD-S14.
- **[OP]** The no-card 30-day trial lowers evaluation friction, and active-member tiers include all features rather than requiring feature selection. See GD-S2.
- **[OS]** Martial-arts terminology and workflows reduce adaptation compared with a generic event platform. See GD-S1.

### Contradicting or limiting evidence

- **[OD]** The guide teaches how to represent a seminar in Gymdesk, not what a first-time organizer must do across venue, instructor travel, promotion, staffing, contingencies, merchandise, day-of operations, and learning. See GD-S3 and GD-R2.
- **[RI]** The product's membership/billing/class-management breadth may be disproportionate for an infrequent organizer who only needs a guided seminar playbook. See GD-S1, GD-S2, and GD-R4.
- **[OP]** No permanent free plan is listed, and the minimum public subscription is `$75 USD/month`; regional processing and SMS costs are unresolved. See GD-S2, GD-S9, and GD-S11.
- **[OD]** Low-friction no-account volunteer task claiming/confirmation was not established. See GD-S7 and GD-S8.

### Important unknowns

- Actual novice setup time and whether terminology is understandable without running a school in Gymdesk.
- Whether seminar configurations can be copied cleanly for infrequent events.
- Whether the help center contains deeper planning guidance not surfaced by the seminar article.
- Whether organizers in Bulgaria can accept payments and communicate at predictable local cost.
- Whether helpers would accept staff accounts or whether the member/booking surfaces could cover their needs.

### Researcher inference

**[RI]** Gymdesk is relevant to Candidate B because it demonstrates that martial-arts seminar enrollment and operations can be documented as a concrete workflow. It is not a close substitute for the proposed explanatory playbook: its guidance is configuration-oriented, its product is broad, and it has no permanent free tier. Relevance remains `Medium`. See GD-S1 through GD-S8, GD-R2, and GD-R4.

**Final Candidate B relevance: `Medium`.**

## 9. Differentiation implications

### Capabilities already covered

- **[OD]** Martial-arts seminar setup, dated access, booking, forms, waivers, capacity/waitlist, payment, participant records, attendance, and check-in. See GD-S3 through GD-S6, GD-S9, and GD-S17.
- **[OD]** Staff permissions, assigned/due tasks, open/done state, communications, member app, browser-based mobile staff access, product stock, and reports. See GD-S7, GD-S8, and GD-S11 through GD-S19.
- **[OP]** Predictable active-member subscription tiers, no-card trial, month-to-month access, and no processor-rate markup. See GD-S2 and GD-S9.

### Capabilities partially covered

- **[OD]** Tasks and dates cover basic ownership/completion/deadlines, but not a seminar workback structure, dependencies, or critical path. See GD-S4 and GD-S8.
- **[OD]** Permissions and internal records create privacy, but not a dedicated organizer-only contingency and decision layer. See GD-S7 and GD-S8.
- **[OD]** Marketing communications, POS stock, and reports cover execution/data but not complete promotion planning, merchandise production workflow, or structured learning. See GD-S11, GD-S12, GD-S16, and GD-S19.

### Credible remaining gaps

- **[RI]** Seminar-first workback guidance explaining what to do, why it matters, when it becomes critical, and how tasks depend on one another. See GD-R2 through GD-R4.
- **[RI]** Structured guest-instructor travel/accommodation and private contingency activation. See GD-R2 and GD-R3.
- **[RI]** Post-event learning connected to a reusable next-seminar plan, with low-burden helper confirmation that can coexist with chat. See GD-R3 and GD-R4.

### Unknowns that are not proven gaps

- Authenticated configuration quality, exact task filters/copying/automation, Bulgarian payments and SMS, offline reliability, and actual integration depth.
- Whether Gymdesk has undocumented templates or customer practices that approximate a seminar project plan.
- Whether target organizers already use Gymdesk, which would materially reduce switching cost.

### Features that should not be copied merely because Gymdesk has them

- **[RI]** Full member CRM, belt/rank tracking, recurring gym billing, access control, payroll, POS breadth, branded app, and growth reporting are club-management capabilities, not validated requirements for a seminar-planning product. See GD-S1, GD-S7, GD-S13, GD-S16, GD-S19, GD-R3, and GD-R4.
- **[RI]** The useful benchmark is the integrated seminar operational boundary, not the entire all-in-one product suite. See GD-S3 and GD-R2.

## 11. Confidence and evidence gaps

- **Overall confidence:** **[RI]** High for public product scope, seminar configuration, tier prices, enrollment, staff permissions/tasks, communications, attendance, and member/mobile boundaries; Medium for regional and real-use conclusions. See GD-S1 through GD-S19.
- **Strongest verified finding:** **[OD]** Gymdesk has a dedicated official one-day/multi-day seminar setup, not merely generic classes or an unsupported inference. See GD-S3.
- **Most important unknown:** Whether its staff-task and document capabilities can become a low-overhead reusable seminar preparation workflow for a target team, rather than an additional operational database alongside chat/Drive.

- **Would an approved future trial materially change the conclusion?** Yes. It could verify setup time, reuse, task depth, permissions, mobile reliability, and duplicate administration. It would not by itself validate demand, select a segment, or authorize implementation.

Its dates, submission mechanics, category wording, and other year-specific rules must be rechecked for a later competition year; this market profile makes no product-design decision based on stale rules. ## 12. Profile conclusion

**[RI]** Gymdesk is a substantial adjacent competitor because it combines martial-arts specialization with a real, documented seminar configuration and broad operational coverage. Its strongest overlap is one-/multi-day enrollment and execution: forms, waivers, booking, capacity/waitlist, payments, participants, attendance, communications, staff tasks, stock, and reports. Its most important unresolved boundary is whether those objects can support reusable long-range preparation without forcing a seminar team into full club administration or duplicate chat/Drive work. The profile keeps `ADJACENT`, Candidate A `Medium`, and Candidate B `Medium`; it does not change the `0 DIRECT` result, select a final segment, or authorize prototype or development work. See GD-S1 through GD-S19 and GD-R1 through GD-R4.
