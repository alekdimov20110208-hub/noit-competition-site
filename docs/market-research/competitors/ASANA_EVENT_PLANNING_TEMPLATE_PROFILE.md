## 6. Candidate A assessment

### Supporting evidence

- **[OS]** The event template covers long-range sections, owners, dates, logistics, budgets, milestones, promotion, day-of work, and run-of-show timing. See AS-S1.
- **[OD]** Starter+ adds dependencies, rules, custom fields, dashboards, milestones, private projects/teams, and guests, supporting multiple responsibilities and controlled collaboration. See AS-S4, AS-S5, AS-S8, and AS-S10.
- **[OD]** Comments, attachments, Drive links, and task templates can retain operational context and reusable task detail. See AS-S6, AS-S11, and AS-S12.

### Contradicting or limiting evidence

- **[OD]** The strongest planning controls are paid and billed per user; the permanent free plan is currently limited to two users per team/project. See AS-S2 and AS-S3.
- **[OD]** Guests reduce paid-seat cost but normally need accounts, while no-account magic links require Enterprise+/Gov. See AS-S7.
- **[OS]** Travel appears only as generic logistics/budget content; no structured instructor, visa, accommodation, pickup, or international guest confirmation workflow is established. See AS-S1.
- **[RI]** No verified contingency cockpit, merchandise inventory, or structured lessons-learned record matches the Candidate A hypothesis. See AS-S1, AS-R2, and AS-R3.

### Important unknowns

- Exact gallery-template task list, field defaults, automation rules, and plan entitlement.
- Ease of adapting the template to a multi-day martial-arts seminar without configuration burden.
- Whether helpers would accept Asana accounts and task interaction instead of responding in current chat/phone channels.
- Field-level privacy for organizer notes within otherwise shared work and any offline event-day behavior.

### Researcher inference

**[RI]** Asana is a strong generic planning substitute for Candidate A. A specialized product would need to beat an established combination of reusable tasks, dependencies, privacy, files, and mobile access, while proving materially better seminar adaptation and lower coordination friction. See AS-S1, AS-S4 through AS-S13, AS-R2, and AS-R3.

**Final Candidate A relevance: `High`.**

## 7. Candidate B assessment

### Supporting evidence

- **[OS]** The official template supplies a suggested event structure rather than requiring a blank project, and it explains several planning components. See AS-S1.
- **[OD]** Tasks can preserve owners, relative dates, dependencies, descriptions, subtasks, collaborators, and attachments; copying/reuse can reduce repeat setup. See AS-S6.
- **[OP]** Personal is permanently free and includes core tasks/projects, common views, storage, integrations, and a two-user current limit. See AS-S2 and AS-S3.
- **[OS]** Native mobile applications and common email/chat/file integrations reduce device and document switching. See AS-S1, AS-S12, and AS-S13.

### Contradicting or limiting evidence

- **[OS]** The template does not establish martial-arts-specific omissions, instructor logistics, or explanations of why each task matters. See AS-S1.
- **[OP]** Dependencies, custom templates, forms, rules, milestones, private projects, and dashboards require Starter+; the free plan's two-user limit constrains a broader helper team. See AS-S2 through AS-S5.
- **[OD]** Ordinary guest participation still requires an Asana account, which may conflict with the low-administration helper behavior described in repository evidence. See AS-S7 and AS-R4.
- **[RI]** Generic project-management concepts may still impose setup and learning work on an infrequent organizer; effectiveness was not directly observed. See AS-R2 and AS-R4.

### Important unknowns

- Whether a new user receives enough embedded rationale to prevent common seminar omissions.
- Whether the public gallery template can be used and reused on Personal without a paid upgrade.
- Actual time to adapt the plan, invite helpers, configure privacy, and maintain it through the event.
- Whether helpers can confirm assigned work with sufficiently little friction.

### Researcher inference

**[RI]** Asana is a high-relevance Candidate B comparator because it combines a ready starting structure with a permanent free tier, yet it does not validate the more specific “what, why, and when” seminar playbook. The product raises the differentiation threshold without resolving the Candidate B demand question. See AS-S1 through AS-S7, AS-R2, and AS-R4.

**Final Candidate B relevance: `High`.**

## 9. Differentiation implications

### Capabilities already covered

- **[OS]** Reusable event structure, tasks, owners, dates, milestones, logistics/budget fields, timeline/checklist/status views, promotion work, and run-of-show timing. See AS-S1.
- **[OD]** Paid-plan dependencies, rules, forms, custom fields, dashboards, privacy, guest access, comments, attachments, and reusable task detail. See AS-S4 through AS-S11.
- **[OS]** Mobile applications and Google Drive integration. See AS-S12 and AS-S13.

### Capabilities partially covered

- **[OD]** External forms can approximate registration, private tasks/projects can approximate an organizer cockpit, and guests can support helpers, but ticketing/capacity, field-level privacy, and no-account low-friction confirmation are not established. See AS-S7 through AS-S10.
- **[OS]** Generic travel, promotion, metrics, and event guidance exist, but instructor logistics, channel execution, lessons learned, and novice seminar rationale remain incomplete. See AS-S1.

### Credible remaining gaps

- **[RI]** Martial-arts-specific sequencing and instructor logistics tied to a seminar timeline. See AS-S1, AS-R2, and AS-R3.
- **[RI]** A private contingency/decision layer plus minimal helper confirmation compatible with current chat/phone habits. See AS-R3 and AS-R4.
- **[RI]** Structured merchandise checkpoints and post-event knowledge transfer rather than generic tasks/status history. See AS-R2 through AS-R4.

### Unknowns that are not proven gaps

- Exact official-template task content, Personal-plan entitlement, offline behavior, and adaptation effort.
- Availability of third-party payment, ticketing, merchandise, or feedback integrations was not exhaustively researched and is not recorded as absent.
- Actual novice comprehension, helper acceptance, and experienced-organizer switching intent remain unvalidated.

### Features that should not be copied merely because Asana has them

- **[RI]** Broad portfolio/goals/workload layers, extensive rule configuration, AI content generation, and enterprise administration are not justified by the repository interviews alone. See AS-S1, AS-S2, AS-R3, and AS-R4.
- **[RI]** Generic project-management breadth is evidence of competitive coverage, not evidence that every feature belongs in a smaller seminar product. See AS-R2.

## 10. Classification review

**Classification result: `KEEP AS ADJACENT`.**

**[RI]** Asana clearly solves a meaningful portion of seminar preparation through a current reusable event template and a mature task-management system. The evidence does not establish martial-arts specialization, a complete seminar playbook, structured instructor/contingency/knowledge-transfer jobs, or target-organizer adoption. It therefore does not meet the repository's `DIRECT` definition, while its high generic overlap makes deprioritization inappropriate. See AS-S1 through AS-S13, AS-R1, and AS-R2.

**Proposed inventory correction:** **[RI]** None for classification or Candidate relevance. Preserve `ADJACENT`, Candidate A `High`, and Candidate B `High`. Add a future note, without editing the inventory in this task, that current Personal is limited to two users and several decision-critical features are Starter+, while the gallery template's exact plan entitlement remains unknown. See AS-S2 through AS-S4.
