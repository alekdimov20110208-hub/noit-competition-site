## 6. Candidate A assessment

### Supporting evidence

- **[OD]** The workback timeline contains tasks, due dates, lead times, statuses, overdue detection, and links to budget/vendor data, supporting long-range and deadline-heavy coordination. See PB-S2.
- **[OD]** Vendor records, call sheets, multi-day run-of-show, creative/production matrices, and a cross-tab hot sheet support a complex event-production workflow. See PB-S2.
- **[OD]** Producer, client, vendor, and creative roles provide differentiated access, and client views hide vendor quote amounts. See PB-S2.
- **[OD]** Reconciliation and retained production history provide a limited post-event learning mechanism. See PB-S2.

### Contradicting evidence

- **[OS]** Public positioning centers professional event and brand-production teams, not martial-arts organizations or volunteer-led seminars. See PB-S1.
- **[OD]** No structured instructor travel, accommodation, private contingency playbook, or martial-arts seminar workflow was verified. See PB-S2.
- **[PO]** Existing research warns that experienced recurring teams may not adopt a formal workspace and may keep urgent communication in phone/chat. See PB-R3 and PB-R4.

### Unknowns

- Whether private notes can be restricted to only the chief organizer or a small trusted subset.
- Whether individual tasks support the ownership and lightweight confirmation behavior required by Candidate A.
- Whether mobile use is practical during travel and event-day operations.
- Whether AI-generated plans handle international martial-arts instructor logistics accurately.
- Whether Planner Basic includes enough collaborators and relevant advanced features.

### Researcher inference

**[RI]** PlanBrite is a strong generic planning comparator for Candidate A, but its professional production model does not establish fit for a martial-arts organization or willingness to replace the organizer's existing method. See PB-S1, PB-S2, PB-R2, and PB-R3.

**Final relevance: `High`.**

## 7. Candidate B assessment

### Supporting evidence

- **[OS]** Brief upload, templates, generated foundational documents, universal workflows, and learning-by-doing positioning overlap a guided first-event setup. See PB-S1.
- **[OD]** The public documentation explains the planning sequence and each workspace's purpose in operational terms. See PB-S2.
- **[OD]** Generated budgets, timelines, vendors, and run-of-show items are editable, reducing the blank-page problem. See PB-S2.

### Contradicting evidence

- **[OS]** The target and vocabulary assume professional producers, budgets, clients, vendors, agencies, and production assets rather than a first-time volunteer martial-arts host. See PB-S1.
- **[OP]** Planner Basic costs `$39/mo` and has a one-event-at-a-time limit; a permanent free plan is not established. See PB-S1.
- **[PO]** Candidate B requires low setup, explanations of what to do and why, and very light helper interaction; those qualities were marketed but not directly observed or seminar-adapted. See PB-R2 and PB-R4.

### Unknowns

- Actual setup time and whether a novice understands generated output.
- AI output quality for a small martial-arts seminar.
- Whether generated advice explains rationale or merely creates event-production records.
- Whether helpers can confirm work without account friction.
- Whether the product can complement rather than replace messaging and Drive.

### Researcher inference

**[RI]** PlanBrite reduces initial document setup but may still be too producer-oriented and expensive for an infrequent organizer. It overlaps Candidate B's onboarding hypothesis enough that specialized guidance must offer more than AI-generated generic tasks. See PB-S1, PB-S2, PB-R2, and PB-R4.

**Final relevance: `Medium`.**

## 9. Differentiation implications

### Capabilities already covered

- **[OD]** AI-assisted generation of budgets, workback timelines, vendor structures, and run-of-show documents. See PB-S2.
- **[OD]** Connected task/deadline/status views, role-aware collaboration, approvals, documents, exports, and event history. See PB-S2.
- **[OD]** Production asset and branded-merchandise tracking at the item/vendor/cost level. See PB-S2.

### Capabilities partially covered

- **[OD]** Restricted collaborator views, contingency/risk surfacing, onboarding, cloud-file bridges, promotion assets, merchandise, and post-event reconciliation are present in general-event form but not verified against seminar-specific jobs. See PB-S2.

### Credible remaining gaps

- **[RI]** A martial-arts seminar playbook that explains domain-specific purpose, sequencing, and omissions remains credible because no such specialization was verified. See PB-S1, PB-S2, PB-R3, and PB-R4.
- **[RI]** Instructor invitations, rank/protocol considerations, travel, accommodation, pickup, preferences, and teaching schedules remain credible specialized gaps. See PB-S2 and PB-R3.
- **[RI]** A deliberately private contingency and knowledge-transfer layer plus minimal helper confirmation remains credible, although actual demand and usability are still unvalidated. See PB-R3 and PB-R4.

### Gaps that are only unknown, not proven

- Mobile usability, external messaging integration, custom event duplication, Basic-plan collaborator limits, field-level privacy, and seminar-specific AI output are `Unknown` rather than absent.

### Features not to copy merely because PlanBrite has them

- **[RI]** A ten-tab professional production universe, margin/revenue tooling, creative and fabrication matrices, AI pricing, and full-stack replacement positioning should not be copied without direct martial-arts seminar evidence. See PB-S1, PB-S2, and PB-R2.

No implementation recommendation is made.

## 10. Classification review

`KEEP AS ADJACENT`

**[RI]** PlanBrite substantially overlaps both candidates' planning jobs, especially Candidate A, but the inspected evidence does not establish martial-arts or seminar specialization, attendee registration, instructor logistics, or adoption by the target organizers. It therefore does not meet the repository's `DIRECT` boundary. Deprioritization is also not justified because its generic planning overlap is decision-critical. See PB-S1, PB-S2, PB-R1, and PB-R2.

## 11. Confidence and evidence gaps

- **Overall confidence: `Medium`.**
- **Strongest verified findings:** **[OD]** connected budget/timeline/vendor/run-of-show model; editable generated records; role-based collaboration; exports; risks; production tracking; reconciliation. See PB-S2.
- **Important unknowns:** mobile behavior, external chat/calendar integrations, seminar and martial-arts adaptation, custom reuse, pricing currency, Basic user limits, feature-plan boundaries, and registration.
- **Inaccessible information:** authenticated workflow behavior, AI outputs, data-import quality, exact permission friction, and any non-public plan comparison.
- **Would direct observation materially change the conclusion?** Yes. A public demonstration or approved future trial could test AI plan quality, edit/reuse behavior, novice onboarding, mobile use, permission boundaries, collaborator friction, and actual plan limits. It would not by itself validate target demand or segment fit.

## 12. Profile conclusion

**[RI]** PlanBrite is a strong adjacent comparator because it already covers much of generic event-production planning, including AI-assisted setup, connected operational records, status/risk visibility, collaboration, exports, and post-event budget reconciliation. Public evidence does not establish martial-arts seminar specialization or the lightweight, private, chat-compatible workflow suggested by the interviews. The profile therefore preserves `KEEP AS ADJACENT`, rates Candidate A relevance `High` and Candidate B relevance `Medium`, does not select a segment, and does not authorize a prototype.
