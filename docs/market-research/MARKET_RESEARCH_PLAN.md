# Market and Competitor Research Plan

## Status and scope

This document defines the next validation stage after PILOT-A1 and INT-A2 through INT-A4. It prepares market and competitor research; it does not contain completed market findings, select a primary segment, approve a product concept, or authorize development, architecture, or prototyping.

Two unresolved primary-user hypotheses remain under comparison:

- **Candidate A:** experienced chief organizers of larger or more complex martial-arts seminars. Possible value includes a private organizer cockpit, long-range preparation, critical deadlines, international guest logistics, private contingencies, knowledge transfer, and promotion and merchandise checkpoints.
- **Candidate B:** first-time or infrequent martial-arts seminar organizers. Possible value includes a guided seminar playbook, explanations of what must be done and why, a reusable timeline, common-omission prevention, lightweight delegation, onboarding, and knowledge transfer.

Neither candidate is selected. All possible value statements remain hypotheses.

The available NOIT regulations apply to the 2025-2026 competition year. Their requirements for analysis of needs and existing solutions support this research stage, but year-specific categories, deadlines, submission procedures, document formatting, and other rules must be checked against the regulations for the actual participation year.

## 1. Research objective

The research must determine:

- which existing products and workflows already solve parts of the reported problem;
- whether Candidate A or Candidate B has the more credible unmet need;
- what differentiated value remains when messaging and Google Drive already work well for some teams;
- whether a specialized martial-arts seminar product is justified;
- which capabilities should and should not enter a future low-fidelity prototype.

The work must test the hypotheses against current alternatives rather than search only for evidence that supports a new product. Completion of this research does not by itself authorize development.

## 2. Competitor categories

The later research must cover at least:

1. Martial-arts school and club management platforms.
2. Martial-arts tournament and event platforms.
3. General event-registration platforms.
4. Event-planning and checklist products.
5. Project and task-management tools.
6. Volunteer and staff-coordination tools.
7. Shared-document and communication workflows.
8. Seminar checklists, templates, guides, and downloadable resources.
9. AI-assisted event-planning tools.

Category coverage is a search requirement, not evidence that every category contains a relevant competitor.

## 3. Direct versus indirect competitors

| Type | Working definition | Inclusion boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Direct competitor | A currently available product that explicitly supports planning or operating martial-arts seminars for one or both candidate segments. | Must overlap substantially with the target user, event context, and primary job. |
| Adjacent competitor | A product serving martial-arts organizations or event organizers that solves a meaningful part of the workflow without specializing in seminar planning. | Must cover a relevant job such as registration, staffing, logistics, recurring plans, or organizer coordination. |
| Substitute workflow | A combination of general tools or habits that organizers realistically use instead of a specialized product. | Examples supported by interview evidence include messaging plus Google Drive, spreadsheets, shared calendars, paper checklists, reusable personal templates, and organizer knowledge held in memory. |
| Informational substitute | A non-software resource that explains what to do, such as a checklist, guide, template, article, course, or downloadable planning resource. | Must provide actionable seminar or event-organization guidance, not only promotion or commentary. |

A product is not a competitor merely because its marketing uses the word “event.” The classification must be justified by target user, job overlap, and realistic adoption.

## 5. Evidence standards

Every future product or workflow claim must record a source, the date checked, and one of these evidence labels:

- **[OS] Official source** - an official product, organization, or resource page.
- **[OD] Official documentation** - current official help, support, or technical documentation.
- **[OP] Official pricing** - a current official pricing or plan-comparison page.
- **[IR] Independent review** - a dated third-party review whose author and context can be identified.
- **[DO] Direct observation** - behavior or functionality directly observed in an accessible product, demonstration, or test account.
- **[RI] Researcher inference** - an interpretation based on cited evidence; it is not a product fact.

Required evidence rules:

- Marketing claims must not be treated as proven usability or effectiveness.
- Feature availability must be checked against current plans, editions, regions, and access restrictions.
- Pricing may change and must always include the date, currency, billing interval, and relevant plan.
- Missing or ambiguous information must be marked `Unknown`.
- Independent reviews must not override newer official information about current features or pricing.
- Direct observation must describe what was tested and its limitations.
- Researcher inference must cite the facts from which it was derived.
- No product should be declared a competitor solely because it uses the word “event.”
- Absence of evidence on a public page must not be recorded as proof that a feature does not exist.

Recommended claim record:

Confidence should be recorded as `High`, `Medium`, or `Low` with a short reason. Official documentation or direct observation can support high confidence; marketing-only wording, outdated reviews, or incomplete access normally reduce confidence.

## 6. Research questions

1. Which existing tools target martial-arts seminars specifically?
2. Which martial-arts management products include event planning?
3. Which products support reusable event playbooks?
4. Which tools provide guided onboarding for a first-time organizer?
5. Which tools support private organizer-only information?
6. Which tools support lightweight helper confirmations?
7. Which products integrate with existing chat or cloud storage?
8. Which products attempt to replace chat and Drive?
9. Is complex dependency visualization common or rare?
10. How do competitors handle travel, accommodation, transport, and guest instructors?
11. Are promotion and merchandise planning part of existing products?
12. Is post-event learning supported?
13. What do existing products charge?
14. What useful functionality is already available for free?
15. Which workflows would be difficult for a specialized product to beat?
16. What unmet needs remain for Candidate A?
17. What unmet needs remain for Candidate B?
18. Which segment appears easier to reach and onboard?
19. Which segment is more likely to pay?
20. What is the smallest differentiated prototype hypothesis justified by the evidence?

## 7. Initial inclusion criteria

Include a product, workflow, or resource when at least one condition is supported by a trustworthy source:

- it directly serves martial-arts organizations;
- it supports event or seminar organization;
- it handles registration, staff, tasks, logistics, or recurring event plans;
- it is a common substitute used by interview participants;
- it provides a relevant checklist or organizational guide.

Record the inclusion reason. Inclusion means “research further,” not “confirmed competitor.”

## 8. Exclusion criteria

Exclude or deprioritize candidates that:

- only manage competitive brackets with no organizational overlap;
- only provide ticket sales with no planning relevance;
- are abandoned or unavailable;
- have no trustworthy source;
- have only superficial keyword overlap;
- are unrelated enterprise systems that the target users would not realistically adopt.

Historical or discontinued products may be recorded separately only when they reveal useful market history. They must be clearly marked as historical and must not be compared as currently available alternatives.

## 9. Planned research outputs

Later research should produce:

- a competitor inventory;
- a detailed comparison matrix;
- a substitute-workflow analysis;
- a Candidate A opportunity assessment;
- a Candidate B opportunity assessment;
- a pricing comparison;
- a feature-gap analysis;
- a differentiation decision;
- a recommendation for the next validation step;
- a decision on whether a targeted INT-A5 interview is necessary;
- a recommendation on whether to proceed to a low-fidelity prototype.

Each output must identify its sources, evidence gaps, contradictory findings, and date of completion.

## 10. Decision rules

The completed research may recommend one of four outcomes:

### `PROCEED TO PROTOTYPE VALIDATION`

Use only when one candidate segment has a credible, recurring unmet job; existing products and substitute workflows leave a specific gap; the proposed value is narrow and testable; and the smallest prototype can test the differentiation without assuming adoption or payment.

### `REVISE SEGMENT OR VALUE PROPOSITION`

Use when a real job remains, but current alternatives cover more of the proposed value than expected, the candidate segments require materially different products, or the remaining differentiation is unclear or too broad.

### `TARGETED INTERVIEW REQUIRED`

Use when market evidence reveals a specific question that existing interviews and public sources cannot answer and that question would materially change the segment choice or prototype scope. The interview must target that named gap; INT-A5 must not be conducted merely to increase the interview count.

### `KILL CURRENT PRODUCT HYPOTHESIS`

Use when credible alternatives already solve the relevant jobs sufficiently, neither candidate shows a defensible unmet need, likely adoption effort exceeds the remaining value, or the only surviving concept is generic task management without specialized value.

Failure to find an identical product is not evidence of demand, differentiation, or feasibility. A prototype must not be authorized merely because no identical competitor is found.

## 11. Research sequence

1. Identify direct and adjacent competitors across all required categories.
2. Analyze the substitute workflows reported by participants.
3. Verify relevant features using official sources and documentation.
4. Verify current pricing, free plans, and important plan restrictions.
5. Compare Candidate A and Candidate B against the same evidence framework.
6. Identify credible differentiation and evidence that contradicts it.
7. Identify remaining evidence gaps and rank them by decision impact.
8. Decide whether a targeted INT-A5 interview is necessary.
9. Recommend or reject low-fidelity prototype validation.

## Research completion checklist

- Every required competitor category has been searched and its coverage recorded.
- Inclusion and exclusion decisions have a short evidence-based reason.
- Every material feature and pricing claim has a label, source, and date checked.
- Unknown information remains explicitly unknown.
- Candidate A and Candidate B are compared separately.
- Existing substitute workflows receive the same seriousness as commercial products.
- Supporting and contradicting evidence are both retained.
- The final recommendation follows one decision rule and explains why the other outcomes were rejected.
- Development remains unauthorized unless a later, explicit approval changes the project boundary.
