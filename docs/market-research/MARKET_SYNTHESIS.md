## 3. What the interviews established

### Repeated evidence

- Important information or activation logic was reported as concentrated in one organizer or individual memory across all four interviews, although role clarity and severity differ. See `docs/validation/INTERVIEW_SYNTHESIS_TEMPLATE.md`.
- Late work, changed conditions, or missing details created additional checking, correction, replacement, or replanning in all four records. The consequences ranged from minor on-site correction to urgent retrieval, rescheduling, or protected schedule continuity. See `docs/validation/INTERVIEW_SYNTHESIS_TEMPLATE.md`.
- Backup arrangements were reported as informal, inconsistently recorded, or retained in memory/chat across all four records. This does not establish that every team lacks adequate fallbacks. See `docs/validation/INTERVIEW_SYNTHESIS_TEMPLATE.md`.
- Phone and chat retained value for urgent coordination, while Drive/shared documents, paper lists, spreadsheets, and memory carried other parts of the process. See `docs/validation/INTERVIEW_SYNTHESIS_TEMPLATE.md`.
- Seminar workflows contained repeatable stages. That repeatability can support reusable structure, but INT-A4 also shows that a mature team may stop consulting a formal responsibility plan after the routine is learned. See `docs/validation/interviews/INT_A3_DECISION.md` and `docs/validation/interviews/INT_A4_DECISION.md`.

### Participant-specific evidence

- INT-A3 described a long-range international seminar process covering approval, invitation, confirmation, announcement, travel, promotion, logistics, schedule preparation, private contingencies, and trusted delegation. Approximately 98% of information, decisions, coordination, and timely activation was reported as depending on the chief organizer. This is one high-relevance participant account, not a prevalence estimate. See `docs/validation/interviews/INT_A3_DECISION.md`.
- INT-A3 also reported late promotion, weak merchandise planning, unsold towels, missed promotional capture, and a delayed instructor covered by a preplanned substitute. These support several possible organizer jobs without proving demand for software. See `docs/validation/interviews/INT_A3_DECISION.md`.
- INT-A4 described an operationally experienced but non-chief helper in a mature recurring team. Messaging and Google Drive worked well, helpers knew recurring tasks and local fallbacks, and an earlier Excel responsibility plan was abandoned after the routine became familiar. See `docs/validation/interviews/INT_A4_DECISION.md`.
- INT-A4 warned that helpers with limited volunteer time may resist extra administration and suggested that a first-time or infrequent organizer could be more plausible than an experienced organizer as a user. The first point is a participant opinion about helper burden; the second is a segment hypothesis, not direct Candidate B evidence. See `docs/validation/interviews/INT_A4_DECISION.md`.
- INT-A2 reported reusable plans, ownership, reminders, shared documents, and several correctable event failures, but its main detailed case was a small music/party event rather than full martial-arts seminar organization. See `docs/validation/interviews/INT_A2_DECISION.md` and `docs/validation/INTERVIEW_SYNTHESIS_TEMPLATE.md`.
- PILOT-A1 supported the broader coordination problem and rejected the generic ReadyGraph framing because another site and manual updates added friction. Its exact seminar role was not recorded. See `docs/validation/interviews/PILOT_A1_DECISION.md`.

### Researcher inference

- Experienced-organizer knowledge concentration may create value when seminars are complex, international, changing, or require handoff, but concentration alone does not create software demand. A successful expert may prefer memory, private templates, calls, and selected files. See `docs/market-research/competitors/ORGANIZER_MEMORY_PLUS_REUSABLE_PERSONAL_TEMPLATES_PROFILE.md` and `docs/validation/interviews/INT_A3_DECISION.md`.
- A first-time-organizer guidance need remains plausible because routines and tacit rationale have to be learned somehow, and another martial-arts group reportedly sought experienced advice. It remains unvalidated because no Candidate B participant has been interviewed or observed. See `docs/validation/INTERVIEW_SYNTHESIS_TEMPLATE.md` and `docs/validation/interviews/INT_A3_DECISION.md`.
- Complex dependency visualization is not supported. The records support prerequisites, timing, fallbacks, and simple links; they do not show demand for a graph or automatic simulation. See `docs/validation/INTERVIEW_SYNTHESIS_TEMPLATE.md`.

### Unresolved claims

- Whether Candidate A would adopt a private planning layer often enough to justify switching or ongoing maintenance.
- Whether Candidate B lacks access to mentors, personal templates, free guides, or club-management/event tools.
- Whether either candidate will adopt, repeatedly use, or pay for a new approach.
- Whether helpers will use a minimal confirmation mechanism outside their existing chat behavior.
- Whether the reported knowledge-transfer risk creates consequential failure when a chief organizer is unavailable. See `docs/validation/INTERVIEW_SYNTHESIS_TEMPLATE.md`.

## 6. Candidate A assessment

### Evidence supporting a meaningful problem

INT-A3 provides strong participant-level evidence of long-range complexity, concentrated activation logic, trusted but informal delegation, private contingencies, instructor logistics, promotion/merchandise weaknesses, and incomplete knowledge transfer. INT-A4 independently reports that the chief instructor retained the complete strategic picture. See `docs/validation/interviews/INT_A3_DECISION.md` and `docs/validation/interviews/INT_A4_DECISION.md`.

### Evidence against routine adoption

INT-A4 reports a mature team that stopped using an Excel responsibility plan once the routine was learned and found messaging/Drive effective. INT-A3 valued phone/chat and personal organizer method, while PILOT-A1 and INT-A2 resisted duplicate platforms and manual status work. See the four decision documents in `docs/validation/interviews/`.

### Incumbent, threshold, and constraints

- **Strongest incumbent substitute:** organizer memory plus reusable personal templates, usually complemented by messaging plus Google Drive and urgent calls. See `docs/market-research/competitors/ORGANIZER_MEMORY_PLUS_REUSABLE_PERSONAL_TEMPLATES_PROFILE.md` and `docs/market-research/competitors/MESSAGING_PLUS_GOOGLE_DRIVE_PROFILE.md`.
- **Switching threshold:** high. A later concept would have to provide visible value beyond flexible private habits without forcing helpers to maintain another system. This is a researcher inference from the incumbent and interview evidence, not measured behavior. See the same profiles and `docs/validation/interviews/INT_A4_DECISION.md`.
- **Privacy/control requirement:** the organizer must be able to retain private contingencies, relationship context, and selected knowledge without exposing the complete playbook to all helpers. See `docs/validation/interviews/INT_A3_DECISION.md`.
- **Helper-administration risk:** substantial enough to test early; no helper confirmation behavior has been observed. See `docs/validation/INTERVIEW_SYNTHESIS_TEMPLATE.md`.
- **Most important unknown:** whether an experienced organizer facing complexity, change, or handoff would externalize and repeatedly maintain selected private knowledge rather than continue with memory/templates/chat/Drive.
- **Current confidence:** `Medium` that a meaningful problem exists in some complex organizer contexts; `Low` that Candidate A would routinely adopt or pay for a new system. This confidence is an evidence judgment, not a statistical estimate. See `docs/validation/interviews/INT_A3_DECISION.md`, `docs/validation/interviews/INT_A4_DECISION.md`, and `docs/market-research/MARKET_COMPARISON_MATRIX.md`.

Knowledge concentration is therefore evidence of a risk and possible job, not automatic demand for software.

## 10. Differentiation constraints

Any later concept would have to prove all of the following before implementation approval:

1. Lower organizer and helper burden than the relevant current workflow, not merely more centralized data.
2. Coexistence with chat, calls, Drive, calendars, registration tools, and personal templates where those already work.
3. Respect for organizer privacy, selective sharing, and control of sensitive contingency or relationship information.
4. Visible value beyond a free general checklist, a generic event template, or mentor-provided artifacts.
5. Usefulness without requiring every helper to create an account or maintain a second status system.
6. A measurable improvement for a named user performing a named job, such as fewer consequential omissions, faster transfer, clearer critical ownership, or more reliable handoff. No improvement is currently measured.
7. A boundary that does not rebuild commoditized registration, ticketing, chat, file storage, or generic task management without evidence. See `docs/market-research/MARKET_COMPARISON_MATRIX.md` and `docs/market-research/MARKET_RESEARCH_PLAN.md`.

## 12. Apply the research-plan decision rule

### Recommendation: `TARGETED VALIDATION`

This recommendation applies the research plan's exact outcome **`TARGETED INTERVIEW REQUIRED`**. That rule is used when market evidence exposes a specific question that existing interviews and public sources cannot answer and that question would materially change segment choice or prototype scope. See `docs/market-research/MARKET_RESEARCH_PLAN.md`.

### Evidence supporting the recommendation

- Candidate A has stronger direct problem evidence but also a strong incumbent workflow, mature-team sufficiency, privacy concerns, and a high switching threshold. See `docs/validation/interviews/INT_A3_DECISION.md`, `docs/validation/interviews/INT_A4_DECISION.md`, and the two substitute-workflow profiles.
- Candidate B has a plausible guidance gap, but the reviewed market already offers free explanatory guidance, generic reusable templates, martial-arts operations products, and low-friction execution tools. See `docs/market-research/MARKET_COMPARISON_MATRIX.md`.
- No actual Candidate B organizer has been interviewed or observed, so the most consequential segment assumption cannot be resolved from the existing four interviews or public sources. See `docs/validation/INTERVIEW_SYNTHESIS_TEMPLATE.md`.

### Evidence preventing a stronger recommendation

- `CONTINUE`/the plan's `PROCEED TO PROTOTYPE VALIDATION` is not justified because no candidate yet combines a credible recurring unmet job, a specific surviving gap, and sufficient segment evidence for a narrow prototype test. Candidate B lacks direct evidence; Candidate A adoption remains unresolved. See `docs/market-research/MARKET_RESEARCH_PLAN.md`.
- `REVISE`/`REVISE SEGMENT OR VALUE PROPOSITION` is premature because the current question is not yet which revised value proposition to select; it is whether Candidate B's assumed guidance gap exists after real alternatives and mentoring are considered.
- `STOP`/`KILL CURRENT PRODUCT HYPOTHESIS` is not justified because participant-level evidence still shows meaningful coordination, knowledge concentration, and possible transfer/guidance problems that have not been tested in the most relevant unresolved segment. See `docs/validation/INTERVIEW_SYNTHESIS_TEMPLATE.md`.

### Exact next action

Conduct **one INT-A5 neutral interview** with an actual first-time or infrequent martial-arts seminar organizer who has completed a seminar recently or is actively planning one. Before discussing any concept, reconstruct the real sequence, ask what guidance/mentor/templates/tools were available, identify a specific consequential omission or uncertainty, record who maintained the plan, and—with explicit consent—review only redacted planning artifacts. Produce an anonymized decision document that separates participant report, participant opinion, researcher inference, and unresolved claims. Do not conduct INT-A5 merely to increase the interview count. See `docs/market-research/MARKET_RESEARCH_PLAN.md` and `docs/validation/INTERVIEW_SYNTHESIS_TEMPLATE.md`.

### What remains prohibited

No segment selection, product concept, MVP, feature list, low-fidelity prototype, architecture, database model, application code, UI, deployment, or development is authorized. A prototype remains prohibited until the targeted evidence is reviewed and a later explicit decision rule supports it.

## 13. Owner briefing — какво трябва да знае авторът на проекта

Проучването показва две едновременно верни неща. Първо, организирането на семинари може да съдържа реална сложност: дълга подготовка, пътуване и настаняване на инструктори, поверителни резервни планове, устно разпределени задачи и знания, концентрирани в един главен организатор. Най-силният пример е INT-A3, но той остава опитът на един много подходящ участник, а не доказателство за целия пазар. Виж `docs/validation/interviews/INT_A3_DECISION.md`.

Второ, настоящите начини на работа не са просто „счупени“. Опитен екип от INT-A4 е спрял да използва Excel плана си, след като е научил рутината, и е работил успешно с messaging и Google Drive. Позвъняванията остават най-бързи при спешни промени, а паметта и личните шаблони позволяват на опитния организатор да пази контрол и поверителност. Виж `docs/validation/interviews/INT_A4_DECISION.md` и `docs/market-research/competitors/MESSAGING_PLUS_GOOGLE_DRIVE_PROFILE.md`.

Пазарното проучване не откри потвърден директен конкурент сред разгледаните решения, но това е ограничена констатация `0 DIRECT`, а не доказателство за търсене. Planning Pod, Asana и PlanBrite вече покриват задачи, срокове, шаблони, зависимости и файлове. Eventbrite, SignUpGenius, Gymdesk и Kihapp покриват регистрация, плащания, присъствие, комуникация и отчетност в различна степен. Безплатната статия `An Event Planning Checklist for Any Event` вече дава широко обяснение „какво и защо“. Най-силният заместител за Candidate A е паметта на организатора плюс личните му шаблони; най-силните алтернативи за Candidate B са безплатното ръководство, Asana, SignUpGenius, Eventbrite и споделен документ плюс чат. Виж `docs/market-research/MARKET_COMPARISON_MATRIX.md`.

Candidate A има по-силни доказателства за проблем, но и по-висок праг за приемане на нов инструмент. Candidate B изглежда правдоподобен, но не е валидиран: нито един реален начинаещ или рядко организиращ човек не е интервюиран. Не е доказано кой би използвал продукта, колко често, дали би платил, или дали софтуерът би бил по-добър от наставник, шаблон и безплатен checklist.

Затова препоръката е `TARGETED VALIDATION`, съответстваща на правилото `TARGETED INTERVIEW REQUIRED`. Интервюто трябва първо да възстанови последния реален процес, наличните наставници, шаблони и инструменти и конкретните пропуски, без да представя продуктова идея. До прегледа на този резултат не се избира сегмент и не се разрешават прототип, MVP или разработка.
